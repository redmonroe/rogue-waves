## 1. What cites, replicates, extends or critiques Häfner et al. 2023? Has the symbolic formula been independently validated on other data?

### Takeaway
I found two direct follow-ups, and neither is a fully independent validation: one is by the original group (Cicon, Gemmrich, Häfner, Jochum, JPO 2024) and one is by an outside group (Azevedo et al., Ocean Science 2026) that reuses the same CDIP/FOWD buoy data at four stations and partly disputes the central predictor. I found no test of the formula on North Sea platforms, ship radar, stereo video or satellite data.

### Cited Findings
- Background (peer-reviewed, 2021): Häfner, Gemmrich & Jochum, "Real-world rogue wave probabilities", Scientific Reports 2021. Crest-trough correlation r is "the dominating parameter in all studied conditions, water depths, and locations", explaining about a factor of 10 in rogue wave risk variation. Data: CDIP buoys processed into the Free Ocean Wave Dataset (FOWD), more than a billion waves. — [Sci Rep 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89359-1); [PMC copy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8115049/)
- Target paper (peer-reviewed, PNAS, published 20 Nov 2023): Häfner, Gemmrich & Jochum derive a symbolic model using causal analysis, deep learning, parsimony-guided model selection and symbolic regression (PySR). Rogue criterion is crest-to-trough height H/Hs > 2.0. Stated drivers: crest-trough correlation, steepness, relative depth, directionality. Steepness has a positive association with rogue waves in deep water and a negative one in shallow water. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2306275120); [arXiv 2311.12579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12579)
- Validation inside the 2023 paper is internal: the data are clustered into subsets and the model is trained on some and tested on held-out ones. The models "outperform existing theory on this dataset in all but one data subset (cnoidal seas)". — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2306275120); [Jochum-hosted PDF](https://www.gfy.ku.dk/~mjochum/rogue2.pdf)
- Data scale reported in press coverage of the 2023 paper: buoys at 158 locations around US coasts and overseas territories, more than a billion waves, about 700 years of records. — [phys.org, Nov 2023](https://phys.org/news/2023-11-ai-formula-monster-years-worth.html); [ScienceDaily, Nov 2023](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/11/231120170956.htm)
- Code and data for the 2023 paper are public; the repository says about 6.2 GB of data is downloaded. — [GitHub: dionhaefner/rogue-wave-discovery](https://github.com/dionhaefner/rogue-wave-discovery)
- Extension by an overlapping group (peer-reviewed, 2023): Cicon, Gemmrich, Pouliot & Bernier, "A Probabilistic Prediction of Rogue Waves from a WAVEWATCH III Model for the Northeast Pacific", Weather and Forecasting 38(11). Rogue probability is highly correlated with r, and r is forecast by a regional WAVEWATCH III model "with moderate accuracy compared with the high performance of forecasting significant wave height". Case study: storm of 21–22 Oct 2021. — [Weather and Forecasting 2023](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/38/11/WAF-D-23-0074.1.xml); [UVic repository copy](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/items/518bf314-2838-4d56-9576-ea48e5b2c25d)
- Extension by the original group (peer-reviewed, 2024): Cicon, Gemmrich, Häfner & Jochum, "An Empirical Model for Skillful Rogue Wave Forecasts", Journal of Physical Oceanography 54(10), DOI 10.1175/JPO-D-24-0013.1. It drives the empirical model with ECMWF global wave hindcast output instead of buoy-derived sea state. Result: "prediction models based on bandwidth effects have the highest log likelihood scores, with the empirical model outperforming all other tested models", and the authors say it "should be considered for operational implementation". — [JPO 2024](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382755795_An_empirical_model_for_skillful_rogue_wave_forecasts)
- Outside-group test (peer-reviewed, published 2 Feb 2026): Azevedo, Marcon, Meyers & Luther (University of South Florida), "Rogue wave indicators from global models and buoy data", Ocean Science 22, 367–386, DOI 10.5194/os-22-367-2026. Uses FOWD/CDIP records at four buoy stations (coverage 2015–2021) against ERA5 and the ECMWF CY47R1 hindcast. Headline: "the crest-trough correlation parameter alone is not a good rogue wave indicator, but its temporal variability is"; rogue waves are usually preceded by a sharp drop in r below 0.5, then a rapid rise above 0.6. — [Ocean Science 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/); [EGUsphere preprint 2025-2031](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-2031)
- One search summary reports that the same study found "75 % agreement" when testing this drop-then-rise ("inverted peak") hypothesis on FOWD buoy data. A second targeted search could not reproduce that phrase, so treat the 75% figure as unconfirmed. — [Ocean Science 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/)
- Public referee comments on the Azevedo preprint are mixed. One referee called the temporal evolution of r a potent predictor. Another said the work overlaps heavily with Cicon et al. (2023, 2024) and that the authors misread Häfner et al., who "do not claim a threshold behaviour of rogue wave occurrences based on crest-trough correlation r>0.6" (the relation is probabilistic, rising with r). A referee also argued that the hindcast's maximum wave height is an expected maximum envelope height and cannot be compared directly with buoy observations. — [Referee/author response AR2](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-2031/egusphere-2025-2031-AR2.pdf); [Peer review page](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/os-22-367-2026-discussion.html)
- A citing-paper statement attributes to Cicon et al. (2023) that r > 0.6 is associated with rogue probabilities "an order of magnitude higher than in uncorrelated wave fields", and describes Cicon et al. (2024) as reducing the empirical formula to a function of r and two empirical constants. — [EGUsphere preprint PDF](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-2031/egusphere-2025-2031.pdf)
- Citation count: the only number I could find is an aggregator listing 7 citations for the arXiv version, which is almost certainly stale. — [Emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/articles/2311.12579)

### Inferences
- The Häfner formula has been extended (model-driven forecasting) and partly simplified (r-only version), but all the checks I found use CDIP buoy data that came through FOWD. Geographic and instrument independence is still missing.
- The single outside-group paper does not test the full symbolic formula. It tests r as an indicator at four stations and concludes the static value is weak, which is in tension with, not a refutation of, the probabilistic claim (a factor-of-10 shift in a roughly 1-in-10,000 event is hard to see at four stations).
- An independent out-of-sample test of the published formula on non-CDIP data (North Sea lasers, stereo video, other national buoy networks) appears to be an open and cheap contribution, given public code and data.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Google Scholar or Semantic Scholar "cited by" lists, so there may be citing papers I missed, especially in ML/symbolic-regression venues where the paper is cited as a methods example.
- I could not retrieve the fitted formula, its coefficients, or the held-out likelihood scores from the PNAS text.
- I found no paper, positive or negative, that applies the formula to North Sea platform data, ship radar or satellite data. Absence here reflects my search, not proof that none exists.

## 2. What newer models exist (2024–2026)?

### Takeaway
New work falls into four streams: the Victoria/Copenhagen empirical-probability line, short-horizon neural-network warning from buoy time series (Maryland), physics-based field studies from the Fedele and Toffoli groups that point in different directions, and a scattering of smaller ML and simulation papers. I found no LLM or agent-driven result on rogue wave probability itself.

### Cited Findings
- Neural network, phase-resolved warning (peer-reviewed, 18 Jul 2024): Breunung & Balachandran (University of Maryland), "Prediction of freak waves from buoy measurements", Scientific Reports, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-66315-3. An LSTM classifies whether a buoy time-series window will be followed by a rogue wave. Training data: 14 million 30-minute surface-elevation samples from 172 CDIP buoys. — [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-66315-3); [UMD news](https://eng.umd.edu/news/story/new-tool-predicts-rogue-waves-up-to-five-minutes-in-advance)
- The Breunung & Balachandran authors frame it as a start: the network allows "only limited conclusions about the fundamental physics", and adding water depth, wind speed and buoy location is suggested as future work. — [EurekAlert release](https://eurekalert.org/news-releases/1051351); [NPR, 18 Jul 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/18/nx-s1-5041762/rogue-waves-deadly-computer-program-predicts-advance-warning)
- Empirical probability model driven by wave-model output (peer-reviewed, 2024): Cicon, Gemmrich, Häfner & Jochum, JPO 54(10), described in section 1. — [JPO 2024](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml)
- Physics-based, linear plus second-order (peer-reviewed, 1 Jul 2025): Knobler, Malila, Tayfun, Liberzon & Fedele, "Effects of bound-wave asymmetry on North Sea rogue waves", Scientific Reports 15, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-07156-6. Data: about 27,500 thirty-minute records (27,505 per Scientific American) over 18 years (2003–2020) from laser sensors on the Ekofisk platform. — [Record with DOI](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-d160ede2c4f84ec887ad2087608039e9); [Georgia Tech release](https://research.gatech.edu/rogue-waves-arent-anomolies-theyre-just-bad-day-sea); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/scientists-might-soon-predict-the-oceans-rogue-waves/)
- Physics-based, nonlinear wind-sea (peer-reviewed, 12 Apr 2024): Toffoli et al., "Observations of Rogue Seas in the Southern Ocean", Physical Review Letters 132, 154101. Stereo camera on the icebreaker S.A. Agulhas II, 28 Jun–13 Jul 2017. Young wind-driven seas showed kurtosis of 3.4 and elevated rogue probability; mature seas showed none. — [PRL](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.132.154101); [arXiv 2310.01841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01841v1)
- Indicator study (peer-reviewed, Feb 2026): Azevedo et al., Ocean Science, described in section 1; it mentions machine learning only as a possible future step. — [Ocean Science 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/)
- Coastal freak waves, a related but different target (peer-reviewed, MDPI JMSE vol. 14 issue 8, which I take to be 2026): "Machine Learning Approaches for Probabilistic Prediction of Coastal Freak Waves" compares Random Forest, SVM and ANN; all reach AUC near 0.80 and accuracy near 74%. It follows a 2020 JMSE ANN model with 12 and 24 h lead times, and states that probabilistic coastal freak wave prediction "remains largely unexplored". — [JMSE 14(8) 689](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/14/8/689)
- Simulation-only ML (preprint, Jun 2025): Hasmi & Susanto, "Model-free Forecasting of Rogue Waves using Reservoir Computing", trained on nonlinear Schrödinger simulations, not ocean data. — [arXiv 2506.21918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21918)
- Wavefield reconstruction (preprint, Aug 2025): physics-informed neural operators for real-time nonlinear wavefield reconstruction from buoys; not a rogue-probability model. — [arXiv 2508.03315](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03315)
- Theory (preprint, apparently Aug 2026): "Rogue Wave Statistics from a Sparse Coherent Structure Decomposition" proposes a closed-form exceedance probability from a log-normal distribution of soliton-like packet amplitudes, for unidirectional seas. I saw only a reference list and an AI-generated summary of it, so this description is low confidence. — [arXiv 2608.09584](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09584)
- Storm case study with phase-resolving simulation (peer-reviewed, Springer): "Characterization of Rogue Wave Occurrence During Typhoon Hinnamnor Using Directional Wave Spectra and High-Order Spectral Simulations", initialised from WAVEWATCH III hindcast spectra and linking rogue probability to skewness and excess kurtosis. — [Springer, DOI 10.1007/s12601-025-00254-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12601-025-00254-6)
- AI-for-science adjacent, not rogue waves (preprint, 2 Oct 2026): Zhou, Wadekar & Zaldarriaga, "How AI Agents Discover Scientific Equations: From Hydrotope Rediscovery to New Water-Wave Amplitudes". A lead agent plus two research agents rediscovered a formula for nonlinear surface-wave scattering and produced a new verified six-point amplitude; only 4 of 18 single-prompt attempts recovered the formula, and conventional and LLM-assisted symbolic regression failed. — [arXiv 2610.00435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00435); [secondary abstract page](https://chatpaper.com/paper/355051)
- General LLM equation-discovery systems exist (SR-Scientist, a physics-guided LLM agent, AgenticEvolve benchmarked on LLM-SRBench), but none of the results I saw apply them to rogue wave data. — [SR-Scientist, arXiv 2510.11661](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11661v2); [Physics-guided LLM agent, arXiv 2602.12259](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12259v1); [AgenticEvolve, arXiv 2610.31326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31326)

### Inferences
- There are two distinct prediction problems in the literature, and they are often conflated in press coverage: (a) sea-state-level probability (Häfner, Cicon, Fedele) and (b) minutes-ahead deterministic warning from a time series (Breunung & Balachandran). The user's question is type (a).
- For type (a), the newest peer-reviewed predictive model I found is still the 2023 formula and its 2024 forecast-mode evaluation. Nothing in 2025–2026 that I found supersedes it with a better-scoring model.
- Agentic equation discovery has reached water-wave theory (scattering amplitudes) in late 2026 but has not been pointed at the rogue-probability dataset, as far as these searches show.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 rogue-probability papers by Janssen, Mori, Onorato, Dematteis, Sapsis, Adcock or Stuhlmeier in these searches. That is a search limitation; these groups are active and likely have relevant work I did not surface.
- I found no transformer-based or foundation-model rogue wave forecasting paper.
- I could not confirm authors or exact dates for the JMSE coastal freak wave paper or the arXiv 2608.09584 preprint.

## 3. How good are current predictions, quantitatively?

### Takeaway
Reported skill is modest and hard to compare across papers: sea-state models shift rogue probability by roughly one order of magnitude between the least and most favourable conditions, and the buoy neural network catches about three in four rogue waves at 1 to 5 minutes, with its operational false-alarm burden not established in what I could read.

### Cited Findings
- Sea-state models: crest-trough correlation accounts for about a factor of 10 variation in rogue wave risk (2021, restated 2023). — [Sci Rep 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89359-1); [PNAS 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2306275120)
- Forecast mode: with ECMWF hindcast input, bandwidth-based models have the highest log-likelihood scores and the empirical model beats all others tested (2024). The score values themselves were not in the snippets. — [JPO 2024](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml)
- Forecasting r from a spectral wave model is only "moderate accuracy", compared with high accuracy for significant wave height (2023). — [Weather and Forecasting 2023](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/38/11/WAF-D-23-0074.1.xml)
- Operational baseline: ECMWF's freak wave warning system is based on nonlinear wave effects and predicts a maximum expected envelope wave height; it does not account for crest-trough correlation. — [JPO 2024](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml); [arXiv 2311.12579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12579)
- Reanalysis and hindcast products underestimate extremes: at four CDIP stations the ECMWF hindcast flagged 301 rogue events against 25 for ERA5 (Hmax at least twice Hs), and skewness, kurtosis and BFI from the models cluster tightly near zero while buoy values are much broader (2026). — [Ocean Science 2026 PDF](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/os-22-367-2026.pdf)
- A referee on that paper stated that skewness, kurtosis and the Benjamin-Feir Index "are not viable parameters to predict real world rogue waves". This is a reviewer opinion, stronger than the paper's own claim. — [Peer review page](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/os-22-367-2026-discussion.html)
- Buoy LSTM (2024): about three out of four rogue waves (75–76%) correctly predicted one minute ahead, and about 73% five minutes ahead. The network was also tested on two buoys at very different depths that were excluded from training. — [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-66315-3); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/18/nx-s1-5041762/rogue-waves-deadly-computer-program-predicts-advance-warning); [UMD Today](https://today.umd.edu/new-umd-tech-predicts-ship-sinking-rogue-waves)
- An earlier conference-style version of Breunung's work reports 80% combined true positives and true negatives with a 9.19% false-positive share; the search tool noted the figures differ between versions and could not say which are final. — [NSF PAR copy, "Are rogue waves predictable from field measurements?"](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10542101)
- Coastal freak wave ML models (2026): AUC about 0.80, accuracy about 74%, with a 50% probability threshold giving the best balance of true and false positives. — [JMSE 14(8) 689](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/14/8/689)
- Second-order bound nonlinearity raises the largest crests by about 15–20% (2025, figure from press summaries of Knobler et al.). — [ScienceDaily, Sep 2025](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/09/250907172647.htm)

### Inferences
- A factor-of-10 spread on a base rate near 1 in 10,000 waves means the best sea-state model moves the per-wave probability within roughly the 1e-5 to 1e-4 range. That is real skill, but most of the variance in whether a given 30-minute record contains a rogue wave remains unexplained chance.
- The 73–76% recall of the LSTM is reported on a classification setup; the paper says it carries over to realistic rare-event conditions, but I could not see a precision or false-alarm rate at the true base rate. With events this rare, even a few percent false positives would swamp true warnings, so that number is the one to verify.
- No common benchmark or agreed scoring rule exists across these papers (log-likelihood in one line, recall/accuracy in another, AUC in a third), which makes "current best" a judgment call.

### Gaps
- Calibration curves, Brier or log scores, and the exact probability range predicted by the 2023 formula: not retrievable from snippets.
- Precision and false-alarm rate of the Breunung & Balachandran network at realistic base rates: not found.
- Any verification of the ECMWF freak wave warning product against observations in 2024–2026: not found.

## 4. Is there a consensus on the dominant mechanism in the real ocean?

### Takeaway
Not settled, but the weight of recent large field datasets (CDIP buoys, Ekofisk, southern North Sea) favours linear superposition shaped by bandwidth, plus second-order bound nonlinearity, over modulational instability. The main recent counter-evidence is the 2024 Southern Ocean result showing strongly non-Gaussian statistics in young wind seas.

### Cited Findings
- Background (review, 2019): Dudley et al., Nature Reviews Physics, state that "the relative importance of linear and nonlinear effects in driving ocean rogue wave generation remains a subject of debate" and flag the limits of 1D models for the real ocean. — [Dudley et al. 2019 PDF](https://groups.oist.jp/sites/default/files/eventattach/2302/Dudley-NatRevPhys-2019.pdf)
- For linear/bandwidth: crest-trough correlation, a bandwidth-related linear quantity, is the dominant predictor in CDIP data (2021, 2023), and bandwidth-based models score best in forecast mode (2024). — [PNAS 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2306275120); [JPO 2024](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml)
- For linear plus second-order: Knobler et al. (2025) report no evidence of modulational instability in 18 years of Ekofisk records; the largest waves are attributed to linear focusing combined with second-order bound nonlinearities. Fedele is quoted as saying modulational instability is mainly accurate when waves are confined in channels, as in laboratory experiments. — [Georgia Tech release](https://research.gatech.edu/rogue-waves-arent-anomolies-theyre-just-bad-day-sea); [phys.org, Aug 2025](https://phys.org/news/2025-08-rogue-demystified-giant-seas-ocean.html)
- Background (peer-reviewed, 2016): Fedele et al. argued the main mechanism is constructive interference enhanced by second-order bound nonlinearities, based on the Draupner, Andrea and Killard waves. — [Sci Rep 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep27715)
- Background (peer-reviewed, about 2022): Teutsch and colleagues concluded modulational instability "did not play a substantial role" in southern North Sea rogue waves (dataset 2011–2016). — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363877167_Rogue_Waves_in_the_Southern_North_Sea-The_Role_of_Modulational_Instability)
- For nonlinear effects in the field: Toffoli et al. (PRL, Apr 2024) found kurtosis of 3.4 in young wind-driven Southern Ocean seas and a return to near-Gaussian statistics once seas are fully developed. Toffoli said more data are needed "to consolidate our finding" and that the physical mechanisms remain to be clarified. — [PRL](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.132.154101); [APS Physics synopsis](https://physics.aps.org/articles/pdf/10.1103/Physics.17.61)
- A 2026 measurement-focused review reports that measured occurrence often exceeds linear Gaussian theory, especially in storms, crossing seas and under wind forcing, with pronounced spatial and temporal clustering; its highlights say wind strongly modulates formation and occurrence probability. — [Ocean Engineering review 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801826007225)
- The Azevedo et al. (2026) paper describes crest-trough correlation as providing "a robust empirical link to rogue wave formation in natural ocean conditions, in contrast to steepness and the Benjamin-Feir Index". — [Ocean Science 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/)

### Inferences
- Among groups working with large in-situ datasets there is growing agreement that BFI and modulational instability are poor predictors for typical directional ocean seas. This is a majority view, not a consensus: the Toffoli result and the 2026 review keep wind forcing and young-sea nonlinearity in play.
- The disagreement is partly about regime. Buoy and platform archives are dominated by coastal and North Sea conditions; the Southern Ocean stereo data sample strongly forced young seas that buoys under-sample. Both sides could be right in their own regimes.
- Measurement type matters too: buoys are known to under-resolve sharp crests, so buoy-based conclusions about wave height (crest plus trough) do not automatically transfer to crest-height statistics, where second-order effects matter more.

### Gaps
- I could not read the 2026 Ocean Engineering review's authors or its own verdict on the mechanism question beyond the abstract highlights.
- I found no 2024–2026 paper that tests the competing explanations head to head on one dataset spanning both regimes.

## 5. How many groups are active? Crowded field or open directions?

### Takeaway
The data-driven sea-state-probability niche is small: I count roughly half a dozen identifiable groups publishing on it since 2023, and most reuse a single buoy archive. Authors and reviewers repeatedly name unexplored directions.

### Cited Findings
- Groups identified in 2023–2026 searches: Victoria/Copenhagen (Gemmrich, Cicon, Häfner, Jochum) — [JPO 2024](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml); Maryland (Breunung, Balachandran) — [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-66315-3); Georgia Tech with Technion and Norwegian collaborators (Fedele, Knobler, Liberzon, Tayfun, Malila) — [Georgia Tech release](https://research.gatech.edu/rogue-waves-arent-anomolies-theyre-just-bad-day-sea); Melbourne-led Southern Ocean team (Toffoli) — [PRL 2024](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.132.154101); University of South Florida (Azevedo, Marcon, Meyers, Luther) — [Ocean Science 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/); a coastal freak wave ML group publishing in JMSE — [JMSE 14(8) 689](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/14/8/689).
- Fedele's models are reported as used by NOAA and Chevron for forecasting where rogue waves are most likely (press claim). — [Popular Science](https://popsci.com/science/how-rogue-waves-work)
- Stated open directions: adding water depth, wind speed and buoy location to the neural network — [EurekAlert](https://eurekalert.org/news-releases/1051351); operational integration of r-based probability with WAVEWATCH III and ECMWF forecasts — [EGUsphere preprint](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-2031/egusphere-2025-2031.pdf); exploring the drop-then-rise pattern in r "in more detail" (referee) — [AR2](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-2031/egusphere-2025-2031-AR2.pdf); more field data in young wind seas (Toffoli) — [APS Physics synopsis](https://physics.aps.org/articles/pdf/10.1103/Physics.17.61).
- The 2026 Azevedo implementation is described as future work, not a live service for mariners. — [USF COMIT post](https://www.marine.usf.edu/comit/2026/08/11/rogue-wave-indicators-from-global-models-and-buoy-data/)
- A 2026 popular article states that no current satellite system can predict individual rogue wave occurrence, and that translating wave-height trends into rogue wave statistics "remains genuinely unresolved". This is a secondary, non-peer-reviewed source. — [New Space Economy, 26 Feb 2026](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/02/26/rogue-waves-and-satellites-how-space-technology-is-changing-what-we-know-about-the-oceans-most-dangerous-phenomenon/)

### Inferences
- The broader rogue wave field (nonlinear optics, integrable systems, laboratory hydrodynamics) is large and crowded. The specific sub-problem here (calibrated P(H > 2Hs | sea state) from real ocean data) is thinly populated, and the lineage of predictive models is essentially one group plus forecasters adopting it.
- The problem is still open in the practical sense: the best published model explains about one order of magnitude of variation, has not been validated outside one buoy network, and has no operational deployment that I could find.
- Plausible places an outsider could add something, based on what authors and reviewers say is missing: (1) independent validation on non-CDIP data; (2) temporal features of the sea state (the r drop-and-recovery signal) rather than static 30-minute summaries; (3) wind and sea-age inputs; (4) a shared benchmark with a proper scoring rule and base-rate-aware evaluation; (5) agentic or LLM-assisted symbolic regression on the public FOWD-derived dataset.
- The main barrier for an outsider is not competition but data and physics subtlety: buoy measurement bias for extreme crests, quality control of spikes, and sampling-variability artifacts in "sea state" parameters estimated from short records.

### Gaps
- I cannot give a defensible count of active groups; the list above is what surfaced in about 30 searches and under-represents Europe and Asia (for example ECMWF, Oxford, Turin, Tokyo/Kyoto, Chinese groups), whose recent work I did not find.
- No benchmark challenge or community competition on rogue wave prediction turned up. I cannot rule one out.

## 6. 2025–2026 developments: datasets, benchmarks, reviews

### Takeaway
The notable 2025–2026 items are the 18-year Ekofisk analysis (2025), the first outside-group reuse of FOWD (2026) and a new measurement-focused review (2026). I found no update to FOWD, no rogue-wave-specific Sofar Spotter or satellite dataset, and no benchmark challenge.

### Cited Findings
- Review (peer-reviewed, Ocean Engineering, 2026; a search summary dates it May 2026): "A review of measured rogue waves: Spatio-temporal characteristics, occurrence probability, physical mechanisms, and predictability". It synthesises satellite altimetry, wave buoy and coastal radar measurements and covers forecasting. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801826007225)
- Dataset in use (2025): Ekofisk platform laser records, about 27,500 thirty-minute records, 2003–2020. — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/scientists-might-soon-predict-the-oceans-rogue-waves/); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/18-year-study-reveals-truth-of-where-giant-rogue-waves-come-from)
- FOWD (background, 2020–2021): built from CDIP buoy records containing over 4 billion wave measurements; published in the Journal of Atmospheric and Oceanic Technology. No extension adding Spotter or other networks was found. — [arXiv 2011.12071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12071)
- Sofar Spotter Archive: a public repository of hourly wave and inferred wind data from the global Spotter network, 2019 to March 2022. Nothing found showing a later extension or a rogue-wave product. — [Sofar Spotter Archive](https://www.sofarocean.com/mx/sofar-spotter-archive)
- Related review outside oceanography (preprint, Mar 2026): Chabchoub, Engels, Kevrekidis, Mistakidis, Katsimiga, Mossman et al., "Extreme (rogue) waves: From theory to experiments in ultracold gases and beyond". It is about ultracold gases and general nonlinear-wave theory, not ocean prediction. — [arXiv 2603.25908](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25908)
- Press framing in 2025: coverage of Knobler et al. presented rogue waves as predictable "bad days at sea" and said many forecasting models still treat them as unpredictable. These are press characterisations. — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/scientists-might-soon-predict-the-oceans-rogue-waves/); [The Debrief](https://thedebrief.org/scientists-finally-unlock-the-mystery-of-rogue-waves-upending-previous-long-held-theories/)

### Inferences
- The Spotter archive holds hourly bulk and spectral data rather than raw surface-elevation time series, so it can supply sea-state predictors (including spectrum-derived r) at global coverage but probably cannot supply rogue wave labels on its own. Pairing it with labelled networks is an unexplored idea as far as I can tell.
- CDIP continues to be the only large open archive of raw displacement time series that this literature uses, which is why independent validation is thin.
