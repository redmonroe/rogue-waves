## 1. What operational products exist, how are they validated, and how skillful are they?

### Takeaway
ECMWF is the only centre I could document as running a named operational "freak wave warning system" (BFI, kurtosis, expected maximum individual wave height from the wave spectrum); I found no evidence of an operational rogue-wave probability product at NOAA/NCEP, the Met Office, or other national services, only research prototypes. Independent evaluations published 2021 to 2026 report that the ECMWF-style envelope/BFI/kurtosis diagnostics have little or no skill at predicting observed rogue-wave occurrence, while a bandwidth-based (crest-trough correlation) empirical model has demonstrated skill in hindcast tests but is not operational anywhere I could find.

### Cited Findings

ECMWF (the reference operational product)
- ECMWF's wave model documentation defines the Benjamin-Feir Index as the ratio of integral wave steepness to relative spectral width; the archived parameter (253) is the square of BFI. Kurtosis is derived from the predicted spectrum in a simplified form because the full expression "is far too involved" for operations; earlier versions ignored angular width so kurtosis depended only on BFI squared; the later system adds a directional-width dependence, and the operational kurtosis is clipped to a fixed range. — [IFS Documentation CY43R1 Part VII](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2016/79992-ifs-documentation-cy43r1-part-vii-ecmwf-wave-model_1.pdf); [IFS Documentation CY47R1 Part VII](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2020/81192-ifs-documentation-cy47r1-part-vii-ecmwf-wave-model_1.pdf)
- "Maximum individual wave height" (shortName hmax, paramId 140218) is a standard ECMWF output in analysis and forecast fields; it is an expectation value, written as ⟨Hmax⟩ = ⟨z⟩·Hs, derived from the 2D spectrum via a maximum wave height distribution; an ECMWF training slide states "If Hmax > 2.2 Hs, it is a freak wave event." — [Bidlot, Ocean wave model output parameters](https://confluence.ecmwf.int/download/attachments/59774192/wave_parameters.pdf); [Bidlot, EUMETrain 2018 slides](http://www.resources.eumetrain.org/data/4/485/mw2018_tuesday_bidlot.pdf)
- A commercial API reseller describes the same parameter as the expected largest single wave "in a record of 20 minutes ... derived from a theory of wave height distribution that includes non-linear effects" (shows the parameter is redistributed downstream as a generic "max wave height" field). — [Meteomatics wave height parameters](https://www.meteomatics.com/en/api/available-parameters/marine-parameters/wave-height/)
- Validation: Janssen and Bidlot, ECMWF Technical Memorandum 588 (May 2009, 42 pp.) extended the freak wave warning system to two-dimensional propagation, introduced average maximum wave height and its period, and presents "a fairly extensive validation of products of the freak wave warning system ... in particular regarding the maximum wave height." (background) — [ECMWF TM 588 record](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/elibrary/75042-extension-freak-wave-warning-system-and-its-verification); [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-the-extension-of-the-freak-wave-warning-system-Janssen-Bidlot/3ea8b0f436a24db564bc23015bc9975c12ef6763)
- A later memo (Janssen, TM 755, 2015) describes updates to the freak wave warning system introduced in IFS cycle 41R1. (background) — [ECMWF TM 755](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2015/10185-notes-maximum-wave-height-distribution.pdf)
- Trade press (2014) characterised the ECMWF product as twice-daily BFI-based forecasts on a roughly 20 km grid and said "if forecast conditions meet the index's standard, a rogue wave warning is issued." This is a journalist's description; I found no evidence of public warnings being issued to mariners on this basis. (background, trade press) — [Maritime Reporter, Sept 2014](https://magazines.marinelink.com/Magazines/MaritimeReporter/201409/content/unpredictable-dangerous-rogue-477859)

Independent skill assessments of the ECMWF-type diagnostics
- Häfner, Gemmrich, Jochum (Scientific Reports 2021, about 4 billion waves from CDIP buoys): crest-trough correlation is "the dominating parameter in all studied conditions, water depths, and locations, explaining about a factor of 10 in rogue wave risk variation"; kurtosis is not useful for forecasting in the studied regime. — [Real-world rogue wave probabilities, Sci. Rep. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89359-1); [EGU OSPP poster](https://www.egu.eu/media/awards/ospp-award/2021/dion_hafner.pdf)
- Häfner et al. (PNAS 2023) state that the crest-trough correlation indicator is not part of ECMWF's operational forecast and that ECMWF's rogue wave forecast "focuses on envelope wave heights which does not account for crest-trough correlation." — [arXiv 2311.12579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12579)
- Cicon, Gemmrich, Häfner, Jochum (J. Phys. Oceanogr. 54, 2153–2164, 2024) tested the Häfner empirical model using ECMWF global wave hindcast input and compared it with the ECMWF freak wave warning system (FWWS): "the expected maximum envelope wave height from the FWWS does not predict the occurrence of rogue waves"; the empirical model "is a skillful predictor and should be considered for operational implementation." — [JPO-D-24-0013.1](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml)
- Cicon, Gemmrich et al. (Weather and Forecasting 38(11), 2023): "Rogue waves cannot be predicted from standard forecasts"; "as of now, there is no robust rogue wave probability forecast"; BFI (used at ECMWF) has little predictive power in the real ocean; crest-trough correlation can be forecast by a regional WAVEWATCH III model "with moderate accuracy"; a sample probability forecast was produced for the 21–22 October 2021 Northeast Pacific storm. — [WAF-D-23-0074.1](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/38/11/WAF-D-23-0074.1.xml)
- Azevedo, Marcon, Meyers, Luther (Ocean Science 22, 367–386, 2026) compared CDIP buoy rogue occurrences (FOWD) with ERA5 and the ECMWF CY47R1 high-resolution hindcast: buoys record far more rogue waves than the model-derived indicators imply; underestimation is attributed to spectral smoothing and spatial averaging. A referee argued spectral models give an expected maximum envelope height, not individual maximum wave height, and that this parameter is uncorrelated with rogue wave height or occurrence. — [Ocean Science 22, 367 (2026)](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/); [peer review file](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/os-22-367-2026-discussion.html)

NOAA / NCEP / US Navy
- NCEP's operational global wave model (GFS-Wave) is WAVEWATCH III one-way coupled to the GFS; the search surfaced no rogue-wave probability or Hmax product in its documentation. — [NCEP/EMC GFS-Wave summary](https://polar.ncep.noaa.gov/waves/Model_Description.pdf); [EMC wave models page](https://www.emc.ncep.noaa.gov/emc/pages/numerical_forecast_systems/wavemodels.php)
- A Naval Research Laboratory memorandum report (2019) describes a prototype WAVEWATCH III utility estimating the relative likelihood of rogue wave development from four contributing factors (including wind forcing and current gradients): a research prototype, not an operational product. — [NRL/MR/7322--19-9953](https://www7320.nrlssc.navy.mil/pubs/2019/orzech4-2019.pdf)
- Space-time extreme (maximum crest/wave height over an area and duration) routines were implemented in WAVEWATCH III by Barbariol, Alves, Benetazzo and Tolman and validated against stereo imagery in the Adriatic; ECWAM bases its extremes on maximum surface elevation and crest-to-trough height distributions; a "unified framework" for WAM and WW3 exists in the literature. These are model capabilities and research products; I found no confirmation they are disseminated as operational NCEP guidance. — [Barbariol et al., space-time extremes in WW3](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287197072_Space-Time_Wave_Extremes_in_WAVEWATCH_III_Implementation_and_Validation_for_the_Adriatic_Sea_Case_Study); [Benetazzo et al., unified framework](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0029801820311847?via=ihub); [Barbariol et al. 2019, global reanalysis](http://faculty.washington.edu/jmt3rd/Publications/Barbariol_2019_globalextremes.pdf)

Other national services
- Météo-France: following the EU MaxWave project (2000–2003), which found ship accidents occurred "mainly in fast changing sea state conditions and in cases of crossing seas," Météo-France developed a cross-sea index used as a warning criterion. (background) — [Rosenthal, Results of the MAXWAVE project](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/PubServices/2005pdfs/Rosenthal.pdf)
- The ExWaMar paper records that rogue-wave warning criteria were implemented in the Météo-France wave forecast system "on a pre-operational basis" (2003), using one index combining directional spreading and Hs and a second being the product of average wave steepness and Hs. (background) — [Bitner-Gregersen et al., Ocean Engineering 292 (2024)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002980182302927X)

### Inferences
- The operational landscape is essentially one theory-driven product (ECMWF) whose headline output is an expected Hmax that scales closely with Hs. Because Hmax ≈ ⟨z⟩·Hs with ⟨z⟩ varying only modestly, the field mostly re-expresses significant wave height; that is consistent with the independent findings that it does not discriminate rogue-prone from non-rogue-prone sea states.
- ECMWF's own validation (TM 588) verified the expected maximum wave height against buoy maxima, which is a different target from verifying a probability of exceeding 2·Hs. A product can verify well on Hmax (driven by Hs skill) while having no skill on the rogue-wave ratio; the 2023–2026 literature indicates this is the case.
- The Cicon et al. 2023/2024 papers show that the inputs needed by the Häfner-type model (spectral bandwidth/crest-trough correlation, steepness, relative depth, directional spread) are computable from existing spectral model output, so producing the forecast is technically cheap. The barrier is not computation.

### Gaps
- I could not read TM 588 itself, so I cannot report its quantitative verification scores (bias, scatter index), the buoy sample, or the exact dates the BFI/kurtosis (commonly given as 2003) and Hmax (commonly given as 2008) parameters went operational. These dates are from my recollection and are unverified.
- I could not confirm the current (2026) IFS cycle's freak-wave formulation or whether ECMWF has responded to the Cicon et al. 2024 recommendation. No source found saying ECMWF has adopted, tested operationally, or rejected the crest-trough correlation approach.
- No source found for an operational rogue-wave product at the UK Met Office, MET Norway, DWD, JMA, or Environment and Climate Change Canada. This is absence of evidence from a limited search, not confirmed absence.
- Whether the Météo-France cross-sea/steepness indices are still produced in 2026 is unverified.

## 2. Do commercial weather-routing providers or shipping companies use rogue wave risk indices in routing decisions?

### Takeaway
I found no primary documentation that any major routing provider (StormGeo, DTN, Sofar, NAPA, WNI) exposes a rogue-wave probability index as a routing constraint, and no evidence of masters or routers acting on one. Documented routing safety logic is built on significant wave height/wind thresholds and ship-motion hazards (parametric and synchronous roll, surf-riding, wave impact); the only claim that StormGeo and DTN use "extreme wave probability products" comes from an unsourced secondary article that I could not open.

### Cited Findings
- StormGeo's onboard BVS (user guide v8.5) flags severe motions such as roll resonance and wave impact using the IMO-proposed method with the vessel's roll period, course, speed and forecast wave trains; risk is tiered against a wave-height threshold, and "BVS will consider Surf Riding and High Waves as a FULL RISK when the wave height is in excess of the defined threshold." No rogue-wave index appears in the retrieved material. — [StormGeo BVS 8.5 user guide](https://www.datocms-assets.com/119923/1713514323-stormgeo-bvs-8-5-user-guide.pdf)
- StormGeo describes its route analysts as factoring in vessel particulars (DWT, LOA, beam, draft, trim, load condition) plus weather and currents; routing is customised by ship type and cargo. No wave-by-wave or rogue-probability input is mentioned. (vendor material) — [StormGeo, route planning for ship types](https://stormgeo.com/insights/route-planning-for-different-ship-types-and-speeds); [Hellenic Shipping News on StormGeo routing](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/the-complex-and-fascinating-world-of-modern-weather-routing-a-deep-dive-into-stormgeos-advanced-weather-routing-services/)
- Sofar Ocean states that in Wayfinder "safety is ensured by restricting possible paths to regions where forecasted wave heights and wind speed remain below captain-specified thresholds"; a 2022 demo shows parametric-roll and synchronous-roll warning areas along routes. (vendor material) — [Sofar, dynamic route optimization](https://www.sofarocean.com/posts/dynamic-route-optimization-finding-the-shortest-path-with-wayfinder?hss_channel=tw-375814528); [Sofar Wayfinder demo](https://www.sofarocean.com/posts/wayfinder-voyage-optimization-demo)
- The U.S. Navy's Fleet Weather Centers are piloting Wayfinder for routing naval vessels under a five-year CRADA signed July 2023; the stated purpose is safe routing, with no mention of rogue-wave products. — [Naval Meteorology and Oceanography Command](https://www.cnmoc.usff.navy.mil/Press-Room/News-Stories/Article/3967747/us-navy-pilots-sofars-wayfinder-to-enhance-safe-routing-at-sea/)
- Unverified secondary claim: a February 2026 web article states that StormGeo and DTN "incorporate extreme wave probability products into their routing services for commercial shipping," that these use the Benjamin-Feir Index, and that BIMCO has published guidance on abnormal wave conditions. The search summary shows no primary source for these statements and I could not open the page. Treat as unconfirmed. — [New Space Economy, 26 Feb 2026](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/02/26/rogue-waves-and-satellites-how-space-technology-is-changing-what-we-know-about-the-oceans-most-dangerous-phenomenon/)
- Design-side evidence of what routing actually does: the revised IACS Rec. 34 (Rev.2, 2022) wave scatter diagram was built from AIS tracks of more than 21,000 cargo ships over 90 m (2013–2020) co-located with hindcast waves, and assigns lower probabilities to the highest sea states than Rev.1; IACS attributes part of the difference to operational behaviour such as voluntary speed reduction, which depends strongly on relative wave heading. — [ISSC 2025 Committee I.1 chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-2668-0_1); [ClassNK, Update of wave statistics standards](https://www.classnk.or.jp/hp/pdf/research/rd/2023/07_e08.pdf); [IACS Annual Review 2022](https://safety4sea.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/IACS-annual-review-2022-2023_05.pdf)
- Accident investigations frame causation in terms of sea state, ship handling and structure, not rogue-wave forecasts: for El Faro (2015), the NTSB cited a Georgia Tech/INSEAN analysis giving about 1/400 probability of a rogue wave in the last 10 minutes and about 1/130 in the last 50 minutes (crest above 14 m, about 1.6·Hs), and concluded a rogue wave was not a factor in the sinking. — [NTSB MAR-17/01](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/accidentreports/reports/mar1701.pdf); [Fedele et al., Sci. Rep. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-11505-5)
- For Viking Polaris (29 Nov 2022, one death), Norway's NSIA found the ship was struck by a breaking wave which, combined with course and speed, shattered windows; sea conditions "were within the sea states defined in the wave scatter diagram the ship was designed for"; the crew "did not have the sufficient premises for predicting the risk"; the recommendations concern window design standards and reinforcement, not forecasting. — [NSIA report 2023/06](https://nsia.no/Marine/Published-reports/2023-06); [Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/report-windows-aboard-expedition-cruise-ship-couldn-t-resist-fatal-wave); [Marine Log](https://www.marinelog.com/legal/safety-and-security/video-nsia-reports-on-deadly-viking-polaris-encounter-with-a-breaking-wave/)

### Inferences
- The documented decision variables in routing are sea-state thresholds set by the master or operator and ship-specific motion risk. A rogue-wave probability conditional on sea state would only change a route if it altered the ranking of candidate routes beyond what Hs, period, direction and crossing-sea information already do. Since routing already avoids high Hs (and Rec. 34 Rev.2 shows the fleet demonstrably does), the incremental decision value lies mainly in moderate sea states with unusually narrow-banded or crossing spectra, where a 2·Hs wave is still small in absolute terms for a large ship.
- The absence of any vendor marketing a rogue-wave index is itself informative: these firms advertise safety features aggressively (roll warnings, wave-impact alerts), so a rogue-risk layer would likely be advertised if it existed and customers valued it.
- The most plausible consumers of a relative rogue-risk layer are smaller or more vulnerable vessels (fishing, yachts, expedition cruise ships with large glazing, vessels with low freeboard) rather than deep-sea cargo ships.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation found (either way) for DTN (SPOS/Vessel Routing), Weathernews (WNI), or NAPA regarding rogue/freak-wave or crossing-sea risk indices. Not verified.
- The BIMCO "abnormal wave" guidance claimed by the secondary article was not located.
- No survey or testimony found on whether masters consult ECMWF Hmax or BFI fields; no trade-press item (Lloyd's List, Riviera, Splash247, gCaptain) found describing a routing decision driven by a rogue-wave index.

## 3. How do design standards treat rogue waves, and did rules change after Draupner or later incidents?

### Takeaway
Design codes do not use sea-state-conditional rogue-wave probabilities. They use long-term extreme statistics (100-year and, in the Norwegian/ISO offshore regime, 10,000-year "abnormal" crest and load levels) combined with short-term crest distributions, which the standards community regards as largely absorbing rogue waves; DNV-RP-C205 explicitly says neither the probability nor the physics of freak waves is settled. Rule changes after incidents have targeted structural details and load calculation methods (air gap, horizontal wave impact, window strength), not forecasting or rogue-specific probability models.

### Cited Findings
- DNV-RP-C205 contains a freak-wave subsection (3.5.13). Text in older editions and repeated in the 2019 edition: the existence of freak waves is not questioned, but "neither the probability of occurrence of these waves nor their physics is well understood" and "no consensus has been reached neither about a definition of a freak event nor about the probability of occurrence of freak waves." The 2019 edition (amended 2021) lists "[3.5.13] New subsection on freak waves" in its change table and characterises a freak wave in a 20-minute record by Hmax/Hs > 2 and/or Cmax/Hs > 1.3. Mechanisms listed include wave-current interaction, combined seas and wave energy focusing. — [DNV-RP-C205, Oct 2010](https://rules.dnv.com/docs/pdf/DNVPM/codes/docs/2010-10/rp-c205.pdf); [DNV-RP-C205 Sept 2019, amended Sept 2021 (preview)](https://store.accuristech.com/products/preview/2793919)
- A 2019 DNV GL presentation (Bitner-Gregersen) states the Draupner wave (Hmax/Hs = 2.1, Cmax/Hs = 1.55) is used in design practice for offshore structures, citing DNV-RP-C205. — [DNV GL, Rogue Waves: Impact on ships and offshore structures (2019)](https://www.wits.ac.za/media/wits-university/conferences/misgsa/documents/2019/2019%204%204%20%20Rogue%20Waves%20FINAL.pdf)
- Norwegian offshore practice checks two limit states: ULS with a 100-year wave and an accidental limit state (ALS) requiring that a 10,000-year wave does not endanger structural integrity. Haver notes that ALS control for environmental loads "is not common beyond the Norwegian Continental Shelf." (background) — [Haver, Impact of the possible existence of freak waves](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/PubServices/2005pdfs/Haver.pdf); [Rosenthal, MAXWAVE results](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/PubServices/2005pdfs/Rosenthal.pdf)
- NORSOK N-003 and N-004 require a positive air gap for the 10,000-year crest in addition to 1.5 m above the 100-year crest; ISO 19902 adds an alternative margin of 30% of crest elevation if larger than 1.5 m. — [DNV report for BOEM/BSEE (677AA)](https://www.bsee.gov/sites/bsee.gov/files/research-reports//677aa.pdf)
- NORSOK N-003 recommends the Forristall (second-order) crest height distribution as the short-term model. — [NORSOK N-003 copy](https://pdfcoffee.com/norsok-n003-final-with-corrections-pdf-free.html)
- ISO 19901-1 terminology: design "extreme" waves have return periods around 100 years; waves with return periods of roughly 1,000 to 10,000 years are "abnormal" waves. — [OEUK Technical Note HSTN002](https://oeuk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Technical-Note-Hydrodynamic-Loading-.pdf)
- Draupner (1 Jan 1995): crest about 18.5 m in 70 m water depth, Hs about 12 m; the platform had reportedly been designed for a calculated 1-in-10,000-year wave of about 20 m height. (background; second figure from Wikipedia) — [Haver](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/PubServices/2005pdfs/Haver.pdf); [Wikipedia, Draupner wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draupner_wave)
- ISSC 2025 Committee I.1 (Environment) states that rogue waves are "largely accounted for in established design practices," with the caveat that this holds only if they are not frequent enough in relevant sea states to govern integrity. — [ISSC 2025 Committee I.1](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-2668-0_1)
- ISSC 2022 discussion record: the committee "acknowledges that there are rogue waves" and that producing design criteria for them "would require better modeling"; one discussant wrote that "we have not reached a level of understanding to include them in the design process." — [ISSC 2022 discussion volume](https://onepetro.org/snameissc/proceedings-pdf/ISSC22V3/ISSC22V3/D011S001R001/3407218/sname-issc-2022-discussion-vol-3.pdf)
- IACS Rec. 34 Rev.2 (2022) replaces the visual-observation North Atlantic scatter diagram with one from hindcast waves sampled along AIS ship tracks; it covers sea states, spectrum, spreading, heading distribution and speed, applies to ships of 90 m and above in unrestricted service, and is more benign than Rev.1. It is a long-term sea-state (Hs, Tz) description; individual-wave or rogue statistics are not part of it in the retrieved material. — [IACS Rec. 34 Rev.2 text](https://safety4sea.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IACS-Standard-Wave-Data-2023_01.pdf); [IIMS summary](https://www.iims.org.uk/iacs-develops-new-wave-data-scatter-diagram/); [ClassNK technical paper](https://www.classnk.or.jp/hp/pdf/research/rd/2023/07_e08.pdf)
- The International Chamber of Shipping filed comments at IMO MSC 108 on the review of the North Atlantic wave data, showing the revised scatter diagram is under active industry scrutiny. — [ICS submission MSC 108/19](https://www.ics-shipping.org/submission/msc-108-19-comments-on-the-review-of-the-north-atlantic-wave-data/)
- EU and Norwegian research programmes fed design discussions rather than producing adopted rogue-specific rules: EXTREME SEAS (2009–2013) aimed to "upgrade ship design" and "develop warning criteria" and proposed a risk-based approach; CORDIS describes findings as "helping to articulate" new design procedures; a trade article said "the jury is still out" on meaningful progress. ExWaMar (2016–2020) shows how rogue-wave and climate uncertainties "can be incorporated in the risk-based approach used in current design practice of tankers." — [CORDIS, Helping ships adapt to rougher seas](https://cordis.europa.eu/article/id/91347-helping-ships-adapt-to-rougher-seas); [Marine Technology News, Rogue Waves](https://www.marinetechnologynews.com/news/rogue-waves-500543); [ExWaMar, Ocean Engineering 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002980182302927X); [DNV ExWaCli project page](https://www.dnv.com/research/maritime/extreme-waves-project/)
- Incident-driven rule change (offshore): after a wave struck COSLInnovator on 30 Dec 2015 (one fatality, about 17 accommodation windows broken), the Norwegian PSA concluded the regulations had been "too vague regarding the applicable calculation methodology for horizontal wave forces on mobile units"; DNV GL issued OTG-13 (air gap, June 2016) and OTG-14 (horizontal wave impact loads for column-stabilised units); DNV GL assessed 30 active harsh-environment rigs, of which 20 were cleared or needed minor formalities; draft Norwegian amendments reference OTG-13/OTG-14. — [Havtil (PSA) investigation](https://www.havtil.no/en/supervision/investigation-reports/2016/cosl-drilling---coslinnovator---investigation-of-incident-with-fatal-consequences/); [Upstream, DNV GL issues air gap guidelines](https://www.upstreamonline.com/live/1179177/dnv-gl-issues-air-gap-guidelines); [Upstream, clearance for 20 rigs](https://www.upstreamonline.com/live/1204532/dnv-gl-gives-air-gap-clearance-for-20-rigs); [DNV rule development news](https://www.dnv.us/siteassets/images/pdf-documents/rule-development-and-statutory-news.pdf); [Offshore Magazine](https://www.offshore-mag.com/vessels/article/16770172/psa-reassesses-causes-of-norway-rig-fatality)
- Incident-driven recommendation (ships): the NSIA Viking Polaris report found that minimum requirements in IACS class rules (including DNV) do not give a dimensioning pressure sufficient for a breaking wave at that height on the ship side, and recommended stronger window design standards. — [NSIA report 2023/06](https://nsia.no/Marine/Published-reports/2023-06)
- ABS: the only link found is ABS co-funding of MIT research (2016) on short-lead rogue-wave prediction; no ABS rule text on rogue waves was retrieved. — [gCaptain, Warning: Rogue Wave Ahead (2016)](https://gcaptain.com/warning-rogue-wave-ahead-predicting-the-ship-killing-waves/)

### Inferences
- Design is driven by the absolute size of the largest crest or load in the structure's lifetime, obtained by convolving the long-term distribution of sea states with a short-term crest/height distribution. A wave of 2·Hs in a moderate sea is far below the 100-year or 10,000-year crest, so a better estimate of P(H > 2·Hs | sea state) matters to design only if it changes the short-term distribution tail in the severe sea states that dominate the long-term extreme. The Häfner-type finding that rogue probability is controlled mainly by bandwidth, with nonlinear effects secondary, tends to support rather than upset the current practice of second-order (Forristall-type) crest models in storm seas.
- Draupner did not introduce the 10,000-year check (Haver describes the ALS as an existing NCS feature and the platform was reportedly designed to a 10,000-year wave); its documented role is as a reference case in DNV-RP-C205 and as the trigger for research programmes (MaxWave, EXTREME SEAS, ExWaMar). I found no source stating that any code changed its numerical requirements because of Draupner.
- The pattern across COSLInnovator and Viking Polaris is that regulators respond to wave casualties by strengthening local structural requirements and load methods. That is a design-side route which makes the hazard less dependent on forecasting.
- A sea-state-conditional rogue model has one plausible design-side entry point: refining short-term crest/height distributions (including dependence on bandwidth, spreading and depth) used in long-term response analysis and air-gap assessment. That is a multi-year standards process via DNV-RP-C205, NORSOK N-003 or ISO 19901-1 revision, and it would need crest-height (not only crest-to-trough height) statistics.

### Gaps
- I could not read DNV-RP-C205 (2019/2021), NORSOK N-003:2017, ISO 19901-1:2015 or IACS Common Structural Rules directly. Whether the 2019 "new subsection" materially differs from the 2010 text is unresolved (both contain 3.5.13 Freak waves). The exact 10^-4 annual probability wording in N-003 is not quoted from the standard itself.
- No retrieved source documents ABS guidance, IACS CSR text, or UR S11A treatment of rogue waves.
- I could not verify from a primary source that the 10,000-year ALS requirement pre-dates 1995; this is inferred from Haver and the Wikipedia design claim.

## 4. Offshore operations: short-term extreme wave warnings and deterministic phase-resolved prediction

### Takeaway
Offshore operations are planned against significant-wave-height limits with explicit forecast-uncertainty factors (DNV-ST-N001 alpha factors; roughly 1.5 m Hs for crew transfer vessels), not against rogue-wave probabilities. The technology that is actually being bought for wave-by-wave decisions is deterministic, radar-based phase-resolved prediction (for example NextOcean WavePredictor, FutureWaves) giving tens of seconds to a few minutes of warning of vessel motion; it targets quiescent windows and motion peaks and is a different product class from statistical rogue-wave probability.

### Cited Findings
- DNV-ST-N001: weather-restricted operations (reference period under about 72 hours) start only when the forecast is below the operational limit multiplied by an alpha factor, which "allows for weather forecasting inaccuracies"; alpha is smaller for longer operations and larger with better forecasting and on-site wave monitoring; tabulated wave alpha factors are of order 0.5–0.7 for periods up to 72 hours in one excerpt. The forecast must stay below the limit for the planned duration plus contingency. — [NoDynamics summary of DNV-ST-N001](https://nodynamics.com/knowledge-hub/dnv-st-n001-marine-operations/); [IIMS, weather-restricted sea transports](https://www.iims.org.uk/weather-restricted-sea-transports-for-heavy-lift-ships/); [DNV-ST-N001 (2023) copy](https://studylib.net/doc/27896128/dnv-st-n001-2023)
- Offshore wind access: "an upper limit of significant wave height of 1.5 m usually applies for CTV operations"; walk-to-work contracts are "usually defined in terms of operating conditions, based on a significant wave height (Hs)"; one operator found wave period as well as height was crucial; a study found poor correlation between visually reported and measured wave heights. — [Riviera, walk-to-work digital twin](https://www.rivieramm.com/news-content-hub/digital-twin-suggests-walk-to-work-norms-could-be-bettered-58113); [Maritime Executive, Bibby Marine](https://maritime-executive.com/corporate/bibby-marine-services-moves-to-optimize-walk-to-work-operations); [University of Hull repository](https://hull-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/4343520)
- A Memorial University thesis on offshore crew and cargo transfer found "significant windows of time with benign vessel motions during which crew transfers can be conducted – even in extreme seas," suggesting use of motion monitoring/prediction systems. — [Memorial University thesis](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/items/29b01ca2-a451-4a0a-9a43-789f15e39565)

(background) — [ScienceDaily, 2016](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/02/160225140034.htm); [gCaptain, 2016](https://gcaptain.com/warning-rogue-wave-ahead-predicting-the-ship-killing-waves/)

### Inferences
- Operations standards already contain a formal mechanism for crediting forecast quality (the alpha factor rises with better forecasting and on-site monitoring). That mechanism is expressed in Hs. A rogue-probability forecast has no corresponding slot: an operation limited at, say, Hs 2.5 m is designed for the individual maxima expected at that Hs, and a statistical statement that 2·Hs waves are several times more likely this afternoon does not map onto a defined limit unless the operation's criteria are rewritten in terms of individual-wave or response maxima.
- Phase-resolved systems answer "what will the next wave/motion be in 30–180 seconds," which is directly actionable for lifts, gangway connections and helicopter operations. Statistical rogue probability answers "how heavy is the tail in this sea state over the next hours." The two are complementary in principle (the statistical product could inform whether to mobilise or how wide a margin to keep; the deterministic product handles execution), but only the deterministic product currently has paying users.
- For fixed platforms the relevant hazard (wave-in-deck, horizontal impact) is handled by design air gap and, where deficient, by operational restrictions tied to forecast Hs or storm severity; I found no documentation of platforms being down-manned on the basis of a rogue-wave index.

### Gaps
- No source found on fixed-platform or FPSO operating procedures (down-manning, restricting access to lower decks) triggered by extreme individual-wave forecasts as opposed to Hs/storm forecasts.
- No independent performance evaluation of WavePredictor or FutureWaves was retrieved; deployment claims are from vendor and trade announcements.
- Helicopter-deck motion limits (for example CAP 437-type heave/pitch/roll criteria) and their relation to wave forecasts were not researched.

## 5. Has any agency, class society or company adopted or tested the Häfner et al. 2023 PNAS model or similar data-driven models? What do practitioners say about operational value?

### Takeaway
I found no evidence that any forecasting agency, classification society or company has adopted the Häfner et al. model. The only documented test with forecast-system inputs is by the authors' own group (Cicon et al. 2024, using an ECMWF hindcast), which recommends operational implementation; the practitioner-side literature (DNV-led ExWaMar, ISSC committees) is cautious, emphasising sampling variability, lack of consensus, and that design practice already largely covers rogue waves.

### Cited Findings
- Häfner, Gemmrich, Jochum, "Machine-guided discovery of a real-world rogue wave model," PNAS, 20 Nov 2023 (doi 10.1073/pnas.2306275120): a neural-network model predicting rogue-wave probability from sea-state parameters, trained only on buoy observations, distilled by symbolic regression into an equation; rogue-wave probability is expressed as a combination of terms tied to different physical pathways. — [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37983488/); [arXiv 2311.12579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12579)
- University of Copenhagen press material says the algorithm, research and data are publicly available and suggests public authorities and weather services could begin calculating rogue-wave probabilities with it; this is the authors' institution describing potential use, not a report of adoption. (press release) — [University of Copenhagen, 2023](https://science.ku.dk/english/press/news/2023/ai-finds-formula-on-how-to-predict-monster-waves/); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2023-11-ai-formula-monster-years-worth.html)
- Follow-up test with model input: Cicon et al. (JPO 2024) found the empirical model driven by ECMWF hindcast spectra is "a skillful predictor and should be considered for operational implementation," whereas the ECMWF FWWS expected maximum envelope height "does not predict the occurrence of rogue waves." The model "emphasizes bandwidth-controlled linear superposition as the predominant mechanism in crest-to-trough rogue wave generation, while nonlinear effects are a secondary term." — [JPO-D-24-0013.1](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/phoc/54/10/JPO-D-24-0013.1.xml)
- The PNAS paper is cited in the ISSC 2025 Committee I.1 (Environment) report, which also reports the Breunung and Balachandran result (three of four rogue waves predicted one minute ahead) and notes work on improving warning criteria; the same report maintains that rogue waves are largely accounted for in design practice. — [ISSC 2025 Committee I.1](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-2668-0_1); [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/375772010_Machine-guided_discovery_of_a_real-world_rogue_wave_model)
- DNV-led ExWaMar (2016–2020; Bitner-Gregersen, Gramstad, Trulsen, Magnusson, Støle-Hentschel, Aarnes, Breivik; Ocean Engineering 292, 2024) proposed three approaches to warning criteria based on weather-forecast information: integrated wave parameters, coupling a phase-averaged spectral model with a phase-resolving model, and machine learning. — [ExWaMar paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002980182302927X); [DNV ExWaMar project page](https://dnv.com/research/maritime/exwamar.html)
- As summarised in the ISSC literature, ExWaMar concluded that "sampling variability makes it very challenging to identify reliable warning criteria for rogue waves, particularly when temporal point data are employed" and that space-time data should be used because point statistics underestimate crest statistics over areas relevant to ships and structures. — [ISSC Committee I.1 report (Chalmers copy)](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/507633/file/507633_Fulltext.pdf); [ISSC 2025 Committee I.1](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-2668-0_1)
- Bitner-Gregersen and Toffoli (2013 workshop) stated that "development of warning criteria for rogue-prone sea states is called for"; the DNV GL position paper "Rogue Waves – Impact on ships and offshore structures" (No. 05-2015) is the reference industry statement. (background) — [13th Wave Workshop presentation](http://waveworkshop.org/13thWaves/Presentations/G4.pdf); [citation in Ocean Science 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/)
- Häfner et al. 2021 suggest that the common rogue definition (any wave above a relative height threshold) may not be meaningful in practice. — [Sci. Rep. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89359-1)
- The Cicon/Gemmrich group states that crest-trough correlation "should be considered to be included in standard wave forecasts." — [WAF-D-23-0074.1](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/38/11/WAF-D-23-0074.1.xml)

### Inferences
- Three years after publication, the Häfner model's footprint is academic: citations, one hindcast-driven validation by a co-author group, and inclusion in the ISSC state-of-the-art review. There is no documented uptake by an operational centre, class society or routing vendor.
- There are two distinct practitioner communities with different attitudes. Wave forecasters/oceanographers (Gemmrich group) argue the product is ready to trial. The design/class community (DNV, ISSC) has called for warning criteria for a decade but its own project (ExWaMar) concluded reliable criteria are hard to establish from point data, and its headline position is that design already largely covers the hazard.
- The Häfner 2021 remark that the relative-height definition may not be practically meaningful aligns with the practitioner view: what damages ships and platforms is absolute crest height, steepness and breaking, not the ratio H/Hs.

### Gaps
- No statement found from ECMWF, NOAA, Met Office, MET Norway, DNV, ABS, or any routing vendor about evaluating the Häfner model specifically. I cannot rule out unpublished internal trials.
- I could not confirm quantitative details of the PNAS paper (number of buoys and waves, range of predicted probabilities across sea states) from full text. The "factor of 10" variation figure comes from the 2021 Scientific Reports abstract.
- No explicit practitioner quote was found saying rogue-wave forecasting "is not operationally valuable"; the scepticism is expressed indirectly (sampling variability, already covered by design).

## 6. What would it take for a new model to be adopted (validation, lead time, false alarm tolerance)?

### Takeaway
No source I found states formal acceptance criteria for a rogue-wave forecast product; requirements must be inferred from how existing products and standards work. The evidence points to three hurdles: (a) verification against independent observations of what users care about (absolute crest heights over ship- or platform-sized areas, not only H/Hs at a buoy); (b) dependence on forecast spectral shape, which wave models predict only moderately well; and (c) a decision framework in which the forecast changes an action, which today exists for Hs (routing thresholds, alpha factors) but not for rogue probability.

### Cited Findings
- Model-input limitation: crest-trough correlation is forecast by a regional WAVEWATCH III model with only "moderate accuracy." — [WAF-D-23-0074.1](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/38/11/WAF-D-23-0074.1.xml)
- Model-input limitation: ERA5 and ECMWF hindcast indicators underestimate rogue occurrence relative to buoys, attributed to spectral smoothing and spatial averaging; the authors call for wave-field-specific parameters and higher-resolution models. — [Ocean Science 2026](https://os.copernicus.org/articles/22/367/2026/)
- Verification difficulty: sampling variability makes reliable warning criteria "very challenging" to identify with temporal point data; space-time data are recommended. — [ISSC 2025 Committee I.1](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-2668-0_1)
- Size of signal: sea-state dependence explains about a factor of 10 in rogue-wave risk between conditions (2021 buoy analysis). — [Sci. Rep. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89359-1)
- Exposure matters as much as point probability: for El Faro, the encounter probability for a crest above 1.6·Hs rose from about 1/400 (10 minutes) to about 1/130 (50 minutes), and the authors attribute higher odds for a ship to the space-time area it sweeps compared with a fixed point. — [Fedele et al., Sci. Rep. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-11505-5); [NTSB MAR-17/01](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/accidentreports/reports/mar1701.pdf)
- Precedent for crediting forecast skill in standards: DNV-ST-N001 alpha factors are larger when forecasting and on-site wave monitoring are better, i.e. standards reward quantified forecast uncertainty, expressed in the operation's limiting parameter. — [NoDynamics summary of DNV-ST-N001](https://nodynamics.com/knowledge-hub/dnv-st-n001-marine-operations/)
- Precedent for implementation route: ECMWF introduced and updated its freak-wave parameters through technical memoranda and IFS cycle upgrades (TM 588 in 2009; update in cycle 41R1 per TM 755), with in-house buoy verification. — [ECMWF TM 588](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/elibrary/75042-extension-freak-wave-warning-system-and-its-verification); [ECMWF TM 755](https://www.ecmwf.int/sites/default/files/elibrary/2015/10185-notes-maximum-wave-height-distribution.pdf)
- Research proposals for warning systems tend to combine approaches (integrated parameters, spectral plus phase-resolving model coupling, ML) rather than rely on one probability formula. — [ExWaMar paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002980182302927X)
- For minute-scale prediction, published hit rates are 75% at 1 minute and about 70% at 5 minutes at buoys; false-alarm rates were not available in the retrieved material. — [Sci. Rep. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-66315-3)
- A consumer-level explainer (low-authority source) summarises the practical position: mariners can get warnings of dangerous sea conditions and probabilistic estimates of elevated extreme-wave risk, but "not a dependable alert that tells you one specific rogue wave is about to hit your vessel"; no independently validated commercial device with reliable lead time is established. — [itechguides explainer](https://www.itechguides.com/rogue-wave-alerts-at-sea-what-forecasts-can-and-cant-tell-you/)

### Inferences
- Lowest-friction path: publish the model as an additional diagnostic field computed from existing spectral output (as Cicon et al. did with ECMWF hindcast spectra). ECMWF already has a product slot (the freak wave warning system) and a history of revising it by technical memorandum; replacing or supplementing the BFI/kurtosis-based ⟨Hmax⟩ with a bandwidth-based probability is a science-led change that needs no regulatory action. Once in ECMWF or NCEP output, routing vendors and API resellers would inherit it at near-zero cost, as they already do Hmax.
- Base-rate problem (my arithmetic, not sourced): under Rayleigh statistics the per-wave probability of H > 2·Hs is exp(−8) ≈ 3×10⁻⁴, i.e. roughly one wave in 3,000, so such waves occur at a fixed point every several hours in any sea state. A factor-of-10 modulation moves the expectation between roughly "one per day" and "several per hour." A warning keyed to rogue probability alone would therefore be either almost always on or would flag conditions in which most "rogue" waves are small in absolute terms. To be actionable the product must be combined with Hs (probability of exceeding an absolute height or crest elevation over a route segment or time window), which collapses much of its information back into the Hs forecast that users already act on.
- False-alarm tolerance is asymmetric by user. A router can absorb a soft advisory layer (colour shading) at low cost; a marine operations manager cannot stop a lift on a probability that is elevated most of the time. This suggests adoption, if any, would begin as an advisory map layer and as an input to risk screening rather than as a go/no-go criterion.
- For design-side adoption the model would need (i) crest-height as well as crest-to-trough statistics, (ii) validation in severe sea states (where buoy data are sparse and buoys are known to under-record sharp crests), (iii) area/space-time extension, and (iv) acceptance through a revision of DNV-RP-C205, NORSOK N-003 or ISO 19901-1. The stated ISSC 2025 position that rogue waves are largely accounted for means the burden of proof lies with showing current short-term distributions are unconservative in governing sea states.
- Lead time is not the binding constraint for the statistical product: it inherits the wave model's multi-day lead time. The binding constraints are forecast accuracy of spectral shape (bandwidth, spreading) at those lead times, which is documented only as "moderate," and the lack of a demonstrated decision that would change.

### Gaps
- No documented acceptance criteria, WMO/IMO guidance, or agency statement was found specifying validation standards, lead times or false-alarm tolerances for rogue-wave forecast products. All requirements above are inference.
- No published reliability diagram, Brier skill score or false-alarm ratio for the Häfner/Cicon model with forecast (as opposed to hindcast) input was retrieved; I could not read the JPO 2024 paper to extract its skill metrics.
- No cost-loss or decision-value study of rogue-wave forecasts for shipping or offshore users was found.
