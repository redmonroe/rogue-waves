# Mathematical state of the art (as of 26 November 2026): existence of exactly quasisymmetric MHS equilibria, and Grad's conjecture

**How to read these notes.** Every finding carries a verification tag:
- **[full text]** = I downloaded the arXiv PDF and read the relevant passage myself.
- **[abstract]** = I read the arXiv abstract page only.
- **[via X]** = I did not read the original; the statement is how paper X (which I did read) describes it.
- **[snippet]** = web-search snippet only; treat as weak.

All arXiv items dated September-November 2026 are **unrefereed preprints** as of the date of these notes. Sources I could not reach from this environment: Helander's 2014 review (pure.mpg.de blocked), the Simons Foundation and Simons-collaboration web sites (blocked), GitHub (not attempted), Garren & Boozer 1991, Lortz 1970, Bruno & Laurence 1996, Cary & Shasharina 1997 (not on arXiv). For those I rely on how later papers describe them and say so.

**Headline, which changes the framing of the whole question.** Between 21 September and 23 November 2026, Grad's conjecture, in the precise form given by Constantin-Drivas-Ginsberg, was refuted by at least seven preprints from several separate groups (some building on each other), one of them with a Lean 4 certification and most of them with openly disclosed use of frontier AI models. The existence of non-axisymmetric MHS equilibria with *exact quasisymmetry* was explicitly named as still open by Landreman on 28 September 2026, and I found no later preprint that resolves it.

## 1. What exactly are the precise mathematical statements of Grad's conjecture and of the quasisymmetry existence problem?

### Takeaway
Grad's conjecture has no single canonical statement: there is Grad's own informal wording (1967, 1985), the Constantin-Drivas-Ginsberg formalisation (2021, the one the 2026 counterexamples target), a stricter "must be axisymmetric" version (Cardona-Duignan-Perrella), a 2025 refinement requiring ergodic field lines (Drivas-Elgindi-Ginsberg), and a post-counterexample "weakened" genericity version (Burby-Cho-Ruth, October 2026). The quasisymmetry problem likewise splits into weak vs strong quasisymmetry, with vs without isotropic force balance, and volume vs single surface; only the "strong (or weak) QS + isotropic MHS + whole toroidal volume + not a Euclidean symmetry" version is the famous open problem.

### Cited Findings

**MHS system.** The unforced magnetohydrostatic equations on a solid torus Omega are `B x curl B + grad P = 0, div B = 0 in Omega, B.n = 0 on the boundary`; dotting with B gives `B.grad P = 0`, so field lines lie on pressure level sets. [full text] — [Gomez-Serrano, Liehr, Taylor 2026, arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)

**Grad's own words.**
- Grad (1967): "...we find it very unlikely that there exist a general class of toroidal equilibria with smooth p ..."; Grad (1985): "other than the stated symmetric exceptions, there are no families of solutions depending smoothly on a parameter." [via Landreman, who quotes both] — [Landreman 2026, arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742)
- Grad listed "exactly four known symmetries for which smooth toroidal plasma equilibria with nested magnetic surfaces can exist ... two-dimensional, axial, helical and reflection symmetries", and wrote in 1985 that "The proper formulation of the nonexistence statement is that, other than stated symmetric exceptions, there are no families of solutions depending smoothly on a parameter." [via CDG, who quote Grad] — [Constantin, Drivas, Ginsberg, "Flexibility and rigidity in steady fluid motion", Commun. Math. Phys. 385 (2021) 521-563, arXiv:2007.09103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09103)
- Original references: H. Grad, "Toroidal containment of a plasma", Phys. Fluids 10(1):137-154 (1967); H. Grad, "Theory and applications of the nonexistence of simple toroidal plasma equilibrium", Int. J. Fusion Energy 3(2):33-46 (1985). [via bibliography of GLT; originals not read] — [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)

**CDG formalisation (the standard precise statement).** "Conjecture 1 (H. Grad). Any non-isolated and non-vanishing (away from the 'magnetic axis') smooth unforced MHS equilibrium on a (topologically toroidal or cylindrical) domain T in R^3 that has a pressure possessing nested level sets which foliate T has either plane-reflection, axial or helical symmetry." "By an isolated stationary state, we mean that, in some suitably topology, there are no nearby steady states aside from those which correspond to a trivial rescaling or translation of the original. It is possible that no such object exists." [full text] — [CDG 2021, arXiv:2007.09103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09103)

**Stricter version.** "Conjecture 2.5. Let M in R^3 be a toroidal domain. Suppose there exists an MHD equilibrium (X, p) on M where p has toroidally nested level sets. Then, the equilibrium is axisymmetric." [full text] — [Cardona, Duignan, Perrella, "Asymmetry of MHD equilibria for generic adapted metrics", Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 249 (2025), arXiv:2312.14368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14368) (journal reference via GLT bibliography)

**"Axisymmetric or isolated" version.** Peralta-Salas & Slobodeanu paraphrase Grad as conjecturing that equilibria foliated by regular pressure levels around a core closed line "must be axisymmetric or isolated". [full text] — [Peralta-Salas, Slobodeanu 2026, arXiv:2606.13462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13462)

**2025 refinement (ergodic field lines).** After building nonsymmetric steady Euler flows with all orbits closed, Drivas-Elgindi-Ginsberg wrote: "it seems reasonable to stipulate that the Grad's conjecture should include the provision that the vector field has ergodic orbits on nearly all its invariant tori. Such things, Grad clearly states, should be either non-existent or exceptionally rare (isolated) outside of (continuous) Euclidean symmetry." They also note "Grad understood that steady states with all closed field lines could exist outside symmetry". [full text] — [Drivas, Elgindi, Ginsberg, arXiv:2510.02955 (Oct 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02955)

**Post-counterexample "weakened" version (October 2026).** "Recent counterexamples show that Grad's conjecture is false. However, a more practical weakening of Grad's conjecture still stands. It asserts smooth MHS solutions fail to exist for generic plasma boundary shape and profile functions. A significant body of computational work supports the weakened conjecture. Moreover, the recent counterexamples fall far short of addressing the weakened conjecture because they only include finite-dimensional families of plasma boundary shapes." [full text] — [Burby, Cho, Ruth, "Elasticized magnetohydrostatics", arXiv:2610.36241 (26 Oct 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36241)

**Weak quasisymmetry (CDG's statement of the Rodriguez-Helander-Bhattacharjee definition).** A non-vanishing vector field xi tangent to the boundary is a (weak) quasisymmetry of B if `div xi = 0`, `B x xi = -grad psi` for some function psi, and `xi . grad|B| = 0` in T. Condition two implies `B.grad psi = 0` and `xi.grad psi = 0`, so psi is a flux function symmetric under xi. [full text] — [Constantin, Drivas, Ginsberg, "On quasisymmetric plasma equilibria sustained by small force", J. Plasma Phys. 87 (2021) 905870111, arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)

**Strong quasisymmetry.** Replace `xi . grad|B| = 0` by `xi x J = grad(B . xi)`. "When f = 0 [no force] it is this stronger form of quasisymmetry which is equivalent to other definitions in the plasma fusion literature involving Boozer angles"; and "when there is no force and the equilibria are toroidal, strong quasisymmetry is equivalent to Definition 1" (attributed in a footnote to a private communication from M. Landreman). [full text] — [CDG 2021, arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)

**Burby-Kallinikos-MacKay definition (equilibrium-independent).** u is a quasi-symmetry of B if (u,0) is a continuous symmetry of first-order guiding-centre motion for all values of the magnetic moment. Theorem 4.2: this holds iff `L_u|B| = 0`, `L_u beta = 0`, `L_u b^flat = 0` (beta the flux 2-form, b the unit vector along B). B is assumed nowhere zero. "we do not assume that B is MHS." [full text] — [Burby, Kallinikos, MacKay, "Some mathematics for quasi-symmetry", J. Math. Phys. 61, 093503 (2020), arXiv:1912.06468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06468) (journal reference via GLT bibliography)

**RHB weak conditions are strictly weaker than BKM's.** "The conditions presented in the paper are less restrictive than the set recently obtained by Burby et al. ... It is also shown that quasisymmetry implies the existence of flux surfaces, regardless of whether the field corresponds to an MHD equilibrium." [abstract] — [Rodriguez, Helander, Bhattacharjee, "Necessary and sufficient conditions for quasisymmetry", Phys. Plasmas 27 (2020), arXiv:2004.11431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11431)

**Third formulation of weak QS.** xi is a weak quasi-symmetry of B if `[xi, B] = 0, div xi = 0, xi(|B|^2) = 0`. [full text] — [Peralta-Salas, Slobodeanu 2026, arXiv:2606.13462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13462)

**Boozer-coordinate formulation.** Quasisymmetry is `|B| = B(psi, M theta - N zeta)` for integers M, N in Boozer coordinates; N = 0 is quasi-axisymmetry (QA), N != 0 quasi-helical (QH); QS with M != 1 is impossible near the axis. [full text] — [Landreman, Paul, PRL 128, 035001 (2022), arXiv:2108.03711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03711); [Klotz, Duignan, Burby, Meiss 2026, arXiv:2609.09517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09517)

**The existence problem as posed by analysts.** "Problem: Given a toroidal domain T, construct a function psi: T -> R with nested flux surfaces and a divergence-free vector field xi which does not generate an isometry of R^3 and is tangent to the boundary, so that [the quasisymmetric Grad-Shafranov equation, xi.grad|B| = 0, the two nonlinear constraints (1.14)-(1.15)] and xi.grad psi = 0 all hold. It is not clear that there are any smooth solutions psi, xi to the above problem." [full text] — [CDG 2021, arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)

**Quasisymmetric Grad-Shafranov equation.** For an MHS field with quasi-symmetry u, flux function psi, p = p(psi), C = u.B = C(psi): `Laplace psi - (u x v)/|u|^2 . grad psi + (u.v)/|u|^2 C(psi) + C C'(psi) + |u|^2 p'(psi) = 0`, with v = curl u (BKM Theorem 10.5). It reduces to the usual Grad-Shafranov equation for u = r phi-hat. "Solutions of (93), however, do not necessarily give MHS fields": if u is not Killing, it must be supplemented by four first-order quasilinear PDEs, (94)-(97). [full text] — [BKM 2020, arXiv:1912.06468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06468)

**Isotropic vs anisotropic pressure.** With anisotropic (gyrotropic) pressure, force balance reads J x B = div Pi, and in a QS field the cross-surface current vanishes pointwise only if p_par + p_perp is a flux function, forcing `p_par = p(psi) + K(psi)B^2`, `p_perp = p(psi) - K(psi)B^2`. [abstract] — [Fu, Bhattacharjee, arXiv:2607.06903 (July 2026, v. Oct 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06903)

**Volume vs single surface.** "Garren and Boozer and Plunk and Helander show that exact quasisymmetry is possible to achieve on one flux surface while maintaining the MHS force balance ... It is unknown whether or not quasisymmetry can be achieved in a volume." [full text] — [CDG 2021, arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)

**Related overdetermined conditions.** Isodynamic (Palumbo 1968): `B . grad|B|^2 = 0`. Any isodynamic MHS field is weakly quasisymmetric, with xi = f1(P) B + f2(P) curl B. [full text] — [Peralta-Salas, Slobodeanu 2026, arXiv:2606.13462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13462). Omnigenity is a weaker confinement condition of which QS is a special case. [full text] — [Plunk, Landreman, Helander, J. Plasma Phys. 2019, arXiv:1909.08919](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08919)

### Inferences
- The CDG conjecture has three escape hatches (isolated, B vanishing off-axis, non-nested pressure). The 2026 counterexamples close all of them: families (non-isolated), nested regular tori, and in most cases B nowhere zero.
- A Lean formalisation needs to fix one version. GLT's Lean statement formalises "solid torus embedded in R^3, smooth B and P, nested regular pressure tori, exact stabiliser C_N, non-isolated point of a moduli space", which shows that the *statement* side is formalisable in Mathlib today.
- For quasisymmetry the cleanest formal target is the PDE system in CDG's "Problem" or BKM's Theorems 4.2 + 10.5 + (94)-(97): a finite list of first/second-order PDE identities on a solid torus, plus "xi is not Killing".

### Gaps
- I did not read Grad 1967/1985 directly; the quotes are second-hand (two independent papers quote the same 1985 sentence, which raises confidence).
- I did not read Rodriguez-Helander-Bhattacharjee's full derivation closely enough to state their weak-QS conditions in their own notation; I give CDG's and Peralta-Salas-Slobodeanu's restatements.
- Whether "weak" and "strong" QS coincide under isotropic MHS is asserted by CDG on the basis of a private communication, not a cited theorem.

## 2. What are the key rigorous results?

### Takeaway
Before September 2026 the rigorous record was: formal (non-convergent) near-axis expansions showing overdetermination of QS + MHS at third order; rigorous structural restrictions on any quasi-symmetry; existence only with a small force, with anisotropic pressure, without force balance, with current sheets, or with zero rotational transform. After September 2026 there are rigorous smooth (even analytic and closed-form) non-symmetric MHS equilibria with nested surfaces, non-vanishing B, and rational or irrational rotational transform, and one rigidity theorem in the quasisymmetry direction (analytic isodynamic implies axisymmetric). No rigorous existence or non-existence theorem for exact non-axisymmetric quasisymmetric MHS equilibria in a volume exists.

### Cited Findings

#### 2a. Near-axis expansions and overdetermination (formal, not theorems of existence or non-existence)
- **Garren & Boozer 1991** (Phys. Fluids B 3, 2805 "Magnetic field strength of toroidal plasma equilibria" and 3, 2822-2834 "Existence of quasihelically symmetric stellarators"). "They show that in general QS solutions run into the problem of overdetermination when the expansion is carried out to third order in the expansion parameter. (They also show that if one restricts consideration of solutions to those with circular axes, then the overdetermination shows up even earlier, at second order.) While the occurrence of overdetermination cannot, by itself, be regarded as a proof of non-existence of QS magnetostatic equilibria, there has been a tendency in the literature to regard the important finding of Garren and Boozer as strong evidence of the non-existence of global QS solutions." [via Rodriguez & Bhattacharjee, full text; GB originals not read] — [Rodriguez, Bhattacharjee, Phys. Plasmas 2021, arXiv:2008.04715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04715)
- Counting through second order: "the number of scalar phi-dependent unknowns exceeds the number of phi-dependent equations by only one if quasisymmetry is imposed through O((r/R)^2), whereas an axis shape represents two phi-dependent quantities ... Therefore, to achieve quasisymmetry through O((r/R)^2), one needs to solve for part of the axis shape." And: "we have not proved rigorously that solutions exist in which B20 is exactly constant". [full text] — [Landreman, Sengupta, "Constructing stellarators with quasisymmetry to high order", J. Plasma Phys. 85 (2019) 815850601, arXiv:1908.10253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10253)
- First-order construction: fields quasisymmetric to leading order in distance from the axis are parameterised by the axis shape plus three real numbers and obtained from a 1D nonlinear ODE of Garren-Boozer. [abstract] — [Landreman, Sengupta, Plunk, J. Plasma Phys. 2019, arXiv:1809.10246](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10246)
- **Jorge, Sengupta, Landreman**: direct-coordinate (Mercier/Frenet-Serret) near-axis expansion of ideal MHD "at arbitrary order in the expansion parameter"; agrees with the Garren-Boozer inverse-coordinate construction at lowest order. [abstract] — [J. Plasma Phys. 86 (2020) 905860106, arXiv:1911.02659](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02659)
- **Sengupta, Rodriguez, Jorge, Landreman, Bhattacharjee (2024)**: axis expansion to all orders for arbitrary beta; "the double-periodicity of physical quantities in a toroidal domain can be satisfied order by order". Formal; no convergence claim. [abstract] — [arXiv:2402.17034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17034)
- **Rodriguez & Bhattacharjee (2021)**: with anisotropic pressure "it is possible to circumvent the problem of overdetermination and carry out the power-series solutions to higher order. This suggests, contrary to current belief, that the existence of globally quasisymmetric fields is likely if one relaxes the assumption of magnetostatic equilibria with isotropic pressure." [abstract] — [arXiv:2008.04715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04715); companion: [Rodriguez, Bhattacharjee, Phys. Rev. E 104, 015213 (2021), arXiv:2012.02077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02077)
- **Rodriguez, Sengupta, Bhattacharjee (2022)**: "the equations satisfied by weakly quasisymmetric magnetic fields can be solved to arbitrarily high order in powers of the distance from the magnetic axis. This demonstration does not consider force balance ... We do not prove that the expansion converges (it is likely divergent but asymptotic), and thus the demonstration here should not be taken as definitive proof of the existence of global solutions." [abstract] — ["Weakly quasisymmetric near-axis solutions to all orders", Phys. Plasmas 2022, arXiv:2110.13703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13703)
- **Ruth, Jorge, Bindel (2024/25)**: Theorem 3.6: the vacuum near-axis expansion is ill-posed (not continuous with respect to smooth perturbations of the inputs in the stated norms); Theorem 3.8: a regularised version converges for suitably smooth input. Proposition 3.2: a real-analytic vacuum field does have a near-axis expansion converging on a smaller neighbourhood. They add that for QA "the system of equations ... is overdetermined beyond third order ... one cannot generally ask for a convergent QA near-axis expansion", and (written in 2024) "currently, there is no guarantee of solutions of MHS, let alone convergent asymptotic expansions." [full text] — ["The high-order magnetic near-axis expansion: ill-posedness and regularization", arXiv:2411.04352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04352)
- **Weitzner 2014, 2016**: formal expansions of nonsymmetric equilibria to all orders in doubly periodic domains and about a circular axis; "his solutions have only finitely many derivatives and neither comes with a proof of convergence". [via de Lacy & Sato] — [arXiv:2611.16688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16688)

#### 2b. Structure and rigidity theorems for quasisymmetry
- **BKM 2020** [full text] — [arXiv:1912.06468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06468):
  - Theorem 7.2/7.3: for a quasi-symmetry u with flux function psi, L_u g is confined to a codimension-4 subspace of symmetric tensors; "A quasi-symmetry u is a Killing field iff L_u u^flat = 0" (in vector form w := curl u x u + grad|u|^2 = 0). "We have not managed to prove or disprove that a quasi-symmetry is a Killing field yet, but the following theorem gets two-thirds of the way".
  - Theorem 8.2: under mild conditions the u-lines on a flux surface are closed, of equal period, with a common rational winding ratio on an interval of flux surfaces (a quasi-symmetry generates a circle action locally).
  - Theorem 9.3: if u is a quasi-symmetry of an MHS field with p constant on flux surfaces then u.B is constant on flux surfaces.
  - Theorem 10.7: for an MHS field with p = p(psi) to be quasi-symmetric, u must satisfy the scalar condition `u x V1 . V2 = 0`, where V1, V2 are explicit vector fields built from u, curl u, w. "The condition of the above theorem merits analysis ... And it would be good to also remove the MHS condition."
  - Motivation: "Perturbative calculations of [GB], however, make it look very likely that the only possibility for exact quasi-symmetry for MHS fields with bounded magnetic surfaces is axisymmetry. Our paper gives first steps to deciding whether or not this is true."
- **BKM 2021**, "Approximate symmetries of guiding-centre motion" (J. Phys. A): approximate *phase-space* symmetries give weaker conditions that "recover 'weak quasisymmetry' as a subcase". [abstract] — [arXiv:2010.08968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08968)
- **Burby, Kallinikos, MacKay, Perrella, Pfefferle (2024)**: "We solve 'half' the problem of finding three-dimensional quasisymmetric magnetic fields that do not necessarily satisfy force balance ... determining which hidden symmetries are admissible as quasisymmetries, and then showing explicitly how to construct quasisymmetric magnetic fields given an admissible symmetry. The admissibility conditions take the form of a system of overdetermined nonlinear partial differential equations involving second derivatives of the symmetry's infinitesimal generator." [abstract] — ["Characterization of admissible quasisymmetries", arXiv:2403.03352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03352)
- **Perrella, Duignan, Pfefferle (2023)**: converse Noether-type theorem (first integral implies symmetry) on the "toroidal region"; quick proofs of existence of flux coordinates without assuming MHS or QS. [abstract] — [J. Math. Phys. 2023, arXiv:2303.03191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03191)
- **Klotz, Duignan, Burby, Meiss (Sept 2026)**: using exterior differential systems and Cartan-Kahler, "every positive analytic function is locally realizable" as |B| in Boozer coordinates; "there is no local analytic obstruction to prescribing the field strength in Boozer coordinates; though further restrictions might arise from global geometric or additional physical constraints." They explicitly leave "open the further questions of global realizability and satisfying force-balance conditions." [full text] — ["All field strengths are possible locally in Boozer coordinates", arXiv:2609.09517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09517)
- **Peralta-Salas & Slobodeanu (June 2026)**, Theorem 1.1: a steady Euler flow u in C^1(closure) and real-analytic in a smooth bounded domain, tangent to the boundary, satisfying the localizability condition u.grad Pi = 0 (equivalently u.grad|u|^2 = 0, i.e. the isodynamic condition), with Bernoulli function constant on boundary components and non-critical there, is axisymmetric, and the domain is a rotationally symmetric torus with convex poloidal section (or convex toroidal annulus). "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first symmetry theorem for 3D steady Euler flows." Remark 1.2: "Grad's conjecture holds true in the case of isodynamic (or localizable) analytic equilibria". Remark 1.3: every isodynamic field is weakly quasi-symmetric, and "an isodynamic equilibrium is, in fact, Killing symmetric." The earlier Palumbo-Balzano argument for a weaker statement (assuming B ergodic on almost all pressure levels) "is rather obscure and it contains several gaps and imprecisions". [full text] — ["A symmetry theorem for localizable steady solutions of the 3D Euler equations", arXiv:2606.13462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13462)
- **Cary & Shasharina 1997** (Phys. Plasmas 4, 3323; PRL 78, 647): "assuming irrational rotational transform ... analyticity of F in eta can only be satisfied for quasi-symmetric" fields, i.e. an analytic exactly omnigenous field must be quasisymmetric. [via Plunk-Landreman-Helander, full text; original not read] — [arXiv:1909.08919](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08919)

#### 2c. Quasisymmetry on a single surface, near axisymmetry, or with modified force balance
- **Plunk & Helander 2018**: expansion in weak non-axisymmetry, vacuum. "A large space of solutions is identified, which satisfy the condition of quasi-axisymmetry on a single magnetic flux surface, while (non-axisymmetric) globally quasi-axisymmetric solutions are shown to not exist, agreeing with the conclusions of previous theoretical work"; "QAS (at a single surface) can be satisfied to all orders in the expansion". (Asymptotic expansion; not a convergence proof.) [abstract + full text] — [J. Plasma Phys. 2018, arXiv:1801.02990](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.02990)
- **Sengupta, Paul, Weitzner, Bhattacharjee 2021**: "While several results have pointed to the existence of exactly quasisymmetric fields on a surface ..., we have obtained the first such solutions using a vacuum surface expansion formalism"; a single nonlinear parabolic PDE; closed-form solutions in cylindrical, slab and isodynamic geometries; numerical quasi-helical local vacuum equilibria near an axisymmetric surface. [abstract] — [J. Plasma Phys. 2021, arXiv:2011.08149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08149)
- **CDG 2021 (small force)**, Theorems 1.1-1.2: for xi a divergence-free field C^{k+2,alpha}-close to the rotation field R e_Phi with closed orbits sweeping out a torus, and a metric g with L_xi g = 0 close to Euclidean, there is a flux function psi solving the generalised Grad-Shafranov equation and a field B with `B.grad psi = 0`, `B x xi = grad psi`, satisfying MHS with a force f bounded by `||f||_{C^{k,alpha}} <= c ||delta - g||_{C^{k+2,alpha}}`. Two of the three QS conditions hold exactly, the third approximately. "We do not address Grad's conjecture here". [full text] — [arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)
- **Sato, Qu, Pfefferle, Dewar 2021**: local quasisymmetric solutions of anisotropic MHD in asymmetric toroidal domains; "the mathematical difficulty encountered in the derivation of global quasisymmetric magnetic fields lies in the topological obstruction toward global extension". [abstract] — [Phys. Plasmas 28, 112507, arXiv:2108.01818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01818)
- **Sato 2022**: "we prove the existence of weakly quasisymmetric magnetic fields by constructing explicit examples ... The obtained solutions hold in a toroidal volume, are smooth, possess nested flux surfaces, are not invariant under continuous Euclidean isometries, have a non-vanishing current, exhibit a direction of quasisymmetry that is not tangential to the toroidal boundary, and fit within the framework of anisotropic magnetohydrodynamics." [abstract] — [Sci. Rep. 12, 11322, arXiv:2204.09241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09241)
- **Sengupta, Nikulsin, Gaur, Bhattacharjee 2024**: at lowest order near axisymmetry and large aspect ratio, 3D quasisymmetric MHD equilibrium reduces to a standard elliptic Grad-Shafranov equation; "any large aspect-ratio tokamak, deformed periodically in the vertical direction, is a stellarator with approximate volumetric QS." [abstract] — [arXiv:2312.08572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08572). Related first-order model that "is generally overconstrained, but special solutions exist": [Nikulsin, Sengupta, Jorge, Bhattacharjee 2024, arXiv:2403.08022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08022) [abstract]

#### 2d. Non-symmetric MHS equilibria before September 2026
- **Lortz 1970** (ZAMP 21, 196-211): toroidal equilibria with nonconstant pressure, plane-reflection symmetric, all field lines closed, rotational transform zero; an iteration-convergence proof, no explicit solution. [via GLT and Landreman] — [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739), [arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742). Enciso et al. add that in Lortz's examples "the pressure level sets fail to define a foliation by nested toroidal surfaces". [via EPP] — [arXiv:2611.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21574)
- **Bruno & Laurence 1996** (Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 49, 717-764): existence of equilibria with piecewise constant pressure in tori that are small perturbations of an axisymmetric one; pressure jumps across finitely many current-sheet interfaces whose rotational transform must be sufficiently irrational. [via GLT] — [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)
- **Enciso, Luque, Peralta-Salas** (J. Eur. Math. Soc. 27 (2025) 2251-2291): piecewise smooth MHD equilibria with piecewise constant pressure and an arbitrary number of current sheets in toroidal domains that "do not need to be small perturbations of an axisymmetric domain, and in fact they can have any knotted topology"; uses a new KAM theorem for divergence-free fields. [abstract] — [arXiv:2104.08149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08149)
- **MacKay 2025**: method to compute regions with no invariant tori for the pressure-jump Hamiltonian in the Bruno-Laurence step. [abstract] — [Phys. Plasmas 32, 052507, arXiv:2501.10353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10353)
- **Salat & Kaiser 1995; Kaiser & Salat 1997**: exact equilibria in an infinite cylinder with straight axis and no cylindrical, helical or mirror symmetry; closed field lines. [via GLT] — [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)
- **CDG 2021 (CMP)**: Liouville-type rigidity theorems (steady solutions without stagnation points in a periodic channel, or axisymmetric solutions in a hollow cylinder, must have further structural symmetry) and flexibility theorems (deform to nearby domains). They state that 3D rigidity outside symmetry "- which is necessary to address Grad's conjecture - seems to be out of reach of existing techniques." [full text] — [arXiv:2007.09103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09103)
- **Cardona, Duignan, Perrella**: an MHD equilibrium with nonconstant pressure on a compact 3-manifold has no continuous Killing symmetries for an open and dense set of adapted metrics. [abstract] — [arXiv:2312.14368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14368)
- **Sato & Yamada 2024/25**: "the primary obstacle in resolving Grad's conjecture ... arises from the incompressibility constraint imposed on the magnetic field." [abstract] — [Physica D 480, 134741 (2025), arXiv:2408.16339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16339)
- **Enciso, Penafiel-Tomas, Peralta-Salas (Jan 2025)**: topology-preserving convex integration gives infinitely many Holder-continuous steady Euler flows with the topology of any given smooth solenoidal field, including on toroidal domains. [abstract] — [arXiv:2501.13632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13632). The same authors later describe this as "counterexamples to Grad's conjecture in toroidal domains of arbitrary knot type ... but the magnetic field is only Holder continuous." [full text] — [arXiv:2611.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21574)
- **Drivas, Elgindi, Ginsberg (Oct 2025)**: smooth steady Euler flows in a periodic cylinder, no continuous Euclidean symmetry, discrete m-fold and plane-reflection symmetry, all orbits closed, foliated by invariant cylindrical level sets of a non-degenerate Bernoulli pressure. [abstract] — [arXiv:2510.02955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02955)
- **Enciso, Kepplinger, Peralta-Salas** (PNAS 122(12), e2414730122, 2025): steady Euler flows on certain closed 3-manifolds that are isolated in C^1, "suggesting that the non-isolation hypothesis in the conjecture is not vacuous". [via GLT] — [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)

#### 2e. The September-November 2026 counterexamples to Grad's conjecture (see section 4 for details)
- Gomez-Serrano, Liehr, Taylor [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739); Landreman [arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742); Issan et al. [arXiv:2610.07304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07304), [arXiv:2611.07672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07672); Lopez [arXiv:2610.29921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29921); de Lacy & Sato [arXiv:2611.16688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16688); Enciso, Penafiel-Tomas, Peralta-Salas [arXiv:2611.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21574); Sengupta et al. [arXiv:2611.31603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31603).

### Inferences
- Nothing in 2a is a theorem about existence: Garren-Boozer is an equation-counting argument in a formal power series. The quoted authors themselves say overdetermination "cannot, by itself, be regarded as a proof of non-existence".
- The only *rigorous* non-existence-type results bearing on quasisymmetry that I found are (i) BKM's necessary conditions, (ii) Plunk-Helander's statement inside a perturbative vacuum expansion, and (iii) Peralta-Salas-Slobodeanu's theorem for the analytic isodynamic sub-case. (iii) is the first genuine rigidity theorem and covers a sub-class of weak QS.
- The only rigorous *existence* results for exact QS drop something: force balance (Sato 2022; Burby et al. 2024; Klotz et al. 2026 locally), isotropy of pressure (Rodriguez-Bhattacharjee, formal), or exactness of force balance (CDG small force).
- "Single-surface QS exists" rests on formal expansions and numerics (Garren-Boozer, Plunk-Helander, Sengupta et al.); I found no theorem with a convergence proof.

### Gaps
- Garren & Boozer 1991, Lortz 1970, Bruno & Laurence 1996 and Cary & Shasharina 1997 were not read directly.
- I did not locate a precise equation/unknown count at third order from a source I read; only the second-order count (Landreman-Sengupta) and the qualitative third-order statement.
- Whether Peralta-Salas-Slobodeanu's theorem extends from analytic to smooth isodynamic fields is not stated in the passages I read.
- The Cary-Shasharina "analytic omnigenous implies quasisymmetric" result is a physics-level argument as far as I can tell; I could not check its rigour.

## 3. What does the numerical evidence say?

### Takeaway
Optimisation gets astonishingly close to quasisymmetry in a finite volume (symmetry-breaking about 5e-5 of the mean field at aspect ratio 6; below 2e-7 at very high aspect ratio), but the authors themselves could not drive the error to machine precision, the error follows Garren-Boozer's cubic aspect-ratio scaling, and the equilibrium codes do not solve the exact MHS equations. The evidence is therefore consistent with an asymptotic near-miss and does not discriminate in favour of exact solutions.

### Cited Findings
- **Landreman & Paul 2022**: for a 1 T mean field with vacuum rotational transform > 0.4, symmetry-breaking Fourier amplitudes B_{m,n} throughout a volume of aspect ratio 6 (QA) or 8 (QH) are <= 50 microtesla, "comparable to the magnetic field of the Earth". Fixed-boundary VMEC optimisation with SIMSOPT, cross-checked with SPEC. [full text] — ["Magnetic fields with precise quasisymmetry for plasma confinement", PRL 128, 035001, arXiv:2108.03711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03711)
- Their own interpretation: "It has been conjectured (but not proven) that in the absence of axisymmetry, QS may be possible on an isolated surface but it cannot be perfectly realized throughout a volume." "More fundamentally, while the results here do not disprove the conjecture that QS cannot be achieved exactly throughout a volume, they do show that one can come surprisingly close in practice. While it did not seem possible in this study to reduce symmetry-breaking errors down to machine precision, the deviations from QS intrinsic in the new QA configuration are small enough to be subdominant to extrinsic sources of field error." And: "QS-breaking errors can always be reduced further by increasing the aspect ratio." [full text] — [arXiv:2108.03711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03711)
- **Landreman & Sengupta 2019**: "the first numerical demonstrations of Garren and Boozer's ideal scaling of quasisymmetry-breaking with the cube of inverse aspect ratio"; a strongly nonaxisymmetric configuration with symmetry-breaking mode amplitudes "< 2 x 10^-7, the smallest ever reported" in a finite volume. [abstract] — [arXiv:1908.10253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10253). Landreman & Paul note this was "only in a narrow volume ... for tori with aspect ratios >= 78". [full text] — [arXiv:2108.03711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03711)
- **Landreman 2019**: core shape and rotational transform of many optimised QS configurations are accurately described by the Garren-Boozer near-axis construction. [abstract] — [Plasma Phys. Control. Fusion 2019, arXiv:1902.01672](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01672)
- **Landreman 2022**: database of 5e5 near-axis-optimised configurations; "Quasisymmetric configurations are observed to exist in continuous bands". [abstract] — [J. Plasma Phys. 2022, arXiv:2209.11849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11849)
- **Landreman, Buller, Drevlak 2022**: precise-QS configurations at significant pressure with self-consistent bootstrap current. [abstract] — [Phys. Plasmas 2022, arXiv:2205.02914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02914)
- **Giuliani, Wechsung, Landreman, Stadler, Cerfon 2022**: coil sets giving fields with excellent quasi-symmetry on computed surfaces; 16-coil configurations with alpha loss < 1%. [abstract] — [J. Plasma Phys. 2022, arXiv:2203.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03753)
- **Nies, Paul, Panici, Hudson, Bhattacharjee 2024**: "stellarators with record levels of quasisymmetry on a surface"; "it remains unclear how broad the parameter space is where good quasisymmetry may be achieved." [abstract] — [J. Plasma Phys. 90 (2024), arXiv:2404.02240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02240)
- **Sengupta et al. 2023/2026 (KdV connection)**: "Given B, it may be possible to deduce an upper bound on the maximum quasisymmetric toroidal volume which depends only on the properties of B. This has been verified for the Landreman-Paul precise quasiaxisymmetric (QA) stellarator configuration." Near the outermost surface B approaches a 1-soliton potential and the connection length diverges. [abstract] — [arXiv:2302.13924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13924); finite-beta follow-up: B on a flux surface "is determined by three or at most four flux functions" [abstract] — [arXiv:2507.06480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06480)
- **Fu, Rodriguez, Conlin, Bhattacharjee 2025** (anisotropic-pressure near-axis code pyAQSC, 6th order): the expansion shows "exponential divergence of the NAE at higher orders" and "is only convergent within a small" volume; measured radius of convergence psi_conv about (9.76 +/- 6.65)e-6 T m^2 for their example. [full text] — [arXiv:2505.20475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20475)
- **Ruth, Jorge, Bindel**: "attempts to use higher-order terms have resulted in divergent asymptotic series ... Most series go to first, second, or sometimes third order ... with any more terms typically reducing accuracy rather than improving it"; in vacuum, the radius of convergence correlates with the distance from the axis to the coils. [full text] — [arXiv:2411.04352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04352)
- **Codes do not solve exact MHS.** "codes like VMEC do not actually solve the MHS equations. Instead they solve implicitly-defined resolution-dependent regularizations of the MHS model." And, as a "theory contradicts computation" list: "Assuming MHS, Garren-Boozer argued quasisymmetric (QS) stellarator equilibria cannot exist. Using VMEC, Landreman-Paul identified numerical equilibria with precise QS in the entire plasma volume. Assuming MHS, Cary-Shasharina show analytic omnigeneity (OM) implies QS. Using VMEC, Goodman et al. find vacuum fields (automatically analytic!) with precise OM and significant deviations from QS." [full text] — [Burby, Cho, Ruth 2026, arXiv:2610.36241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36241)
- **Generic non-smoothness of numerical equilibria.** "In three dimensional toroidal domains without symmetry, the standard ... MHD equilibrium model ... does not generally support smooth solutions. Instead, solutions have singular plasma currents on resonant magnetic surfaces ... leading to the non- or slow convergence of numerical approximations under refinement." [abstract] — [Ruth, Burby, Sengupta, Brown, arXiv:2604.08878 (Apr 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08878)
- **Quasisymmetry and current singularities.** QS confines Pfirsch-Schluter singularities, to leading order, to the single surface resonant with the symmetry helicity. [abstract] — [Rodriguez, Bhattacharjee 2021, arXiv:2105.07807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07807); for anisotropic QS equilibria "a single resonance at iota = N/M in place of the dense set of resonant obstructions of a generic three-dimensional field" [abstract] — [arXiv:2607.06903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06903)
- **Quasi-isodynamic analogue.** Precisely quasi-isodynamic fields found numerically [abstract] — [Goodman et al., J. Plasma Phys. 2023, arXiv:2211.09829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09829); 800,000-configuration near-axis QI database [abstract] — [Rodriguez, Plunk, J. Plasma Phys. 2026, arXiv:2601.08400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08400)
- **New exact benchmarks.** Landreman's closed-form equilibria come with "Scripts confirming that numerical equilibria match these analytic solutions". [full text] — [arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742)

### Inferences
- The residual 50 microtesla / 1 T = 5e-5 at aspect ratio 6 and < 2e-7 at aspect ratio >= 78 fits "error proportional to (a/R)^3 with a small prefactor", i.e. quasisymmetry satisfied through second order in the near-axis expansion and violated at third. That is exactly the Garren-Boozer picture, not evidence of an exact solution.
- Because VMEC assumes nested surfaces and is a resolution-dependent regularisation, numerical QS "equilibria" are not certificates even of exact MHS solutions, let alone exactly quasisymmetric ones. A computer-assisted proof (interval arithmetic around a numerical candidate) would need a well-posed fixed-point formulation that does not yet exist for this problem.
- The Cary-Shasharina vs Goodman et al. tension (analytic omnigenous implies QS, yet analytic vacuum fields are found numerically very close to omnigenous and far from QS) shows that "near-misses to arbitrary numerical precision" coexist with exact non-existence in a neighbouring problem. This is a caution against reading Landreman-Paul as evidence for exact existence.

### Gaps
- No published study that I found pushes QS error down with extended precision or tests whether the floor is nonzero at fixed aspect ratio; Landreman-Paul report only that machine precision "did not seem possible in this study".
- I did not find the PNAS paper by Wechsung et al. ("Precise stellarator quasi-symmetry can be achieved with electromagnetic coils") on arXiv by search; the closely related Giuliani et al. paper is cited instead.
- Sengupta et al.'s "upper bound on the maximum quasisymmetric toroidal volume" is known to me only from the abstract.

## 4. Has anything been resolved or substantially advanced in 2024-2026, including AI-assisted or computer-assisted proofs, or formal verification?

### Takeaway
Yes, dramatically. Grad's conjecture (CDG form) was refuted in September 2026, first by a 147-page Nash-Moser construction certified in Lean 4 with heavy LLM assistance, then within one day by closed-form elementary-function solutions found with an AI model, then by a cascade of extensions (no stellarator symmetry, arbitrary field periods, vacuum, force-free, trivial symmetry group, irrational rotational transform, arbitrary knot type). Nearly every one of these papers discloses frontier-AI use. Exact quasisymmetry is the named problem left standing.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Gomez-Serrano, Liehr, Taylor, "Counterexamples to Grad's conjecture", arXiv:2609.24739, 21 Sept 2026, 147 pp.** [full text of introduction, Section 16, bibliography] — [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)
- Theorem 1.1: Fix L > 0. There are a compact interval I in (0, 1/2) with nonempty interior and N0 such that for every integer N >= N0 and lambda in I there is a smooth embedding X_{N,lambda}: D^2 x T -> R^3 and smooth B, P on its image solving MHS with: (i) the critical set of P is the round circle of radius NL and every regular pressure level is an embedded torus, these foliating the complement of the axis; (ii) B vanishes exactly on the axis; (iii) the extended stabiliser (Euclidean isometries preserving the domain and P and mapping B to +/-B) is exactly the cyclic group C_N; (iv) for fixed N the map lambda -> [configuration] into the moduli space of embedded configurations is continuous and injective for every finite k >= 3 and in C^infinity, so each interior member is non-isolated.

**(2) Landreman, "Analytic toroidal 3D MHD equilibria and steady Euler flows with invariant surfaces", arXiv:2609.26742, 22 Sept 2026 (v2 28 Sept), "Under consideration for publication in J. Plasma Phys."** [full text] — [arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742)

### Inferences
- The DEG "ergodic orbits" refinement of Grad's conjecture also appears to be dead: EPP's Theorem 1.1 with irrational kappa gives smooth, non-isolated, symmetry-free equilibria with every field line dense on every regular torus, and Landreman's second family is analytic with shear. - What the 2026 counterexamples have in common: either all field lines closed (GLT, Landreman family 1, de Lacy-Sato, Lopez, Issan et al.), which removes small denominators, or rotational transform constant across surfaces (EPP), or a special integrable algebraic structure (Landreman family 2). None is claimed to be quasisymmetric, and none addresses generic boundaries.
- Lean relevance: (a) a full formalisation of a 147-page hard-analysis existence proof in this exact area was completed by a three-person specialist team with LLMs in roughly July-September 2026, so the Mathlib infrastructure for stating MHS equilibria on embedded solid tori exists in at least one public repository; (b) Landreman's first family is verified by direct differentiation of elementary functions, which is a much smaller formalisation target than GLT's theorem. I found no statement that Landreman's, Issan et al.'s, Lopez's, de Lacy-Sato's, EPP's or Sengupta et al.'s results have been formalised.
- The speed of the cascade (two months; "promising candidates appeared within hours") means obvious next steps listed in these papers are being taken by specialists with frontier AI on a timescale of days to weeks.

## 5. What are the explicitly stated open problems, and which are stated as tractable sub-problems?

### Takeaway
The single most prominent open problem, restated after the Grad counterexamples, is the existence of non-axisymmetric MHS equilibria with exact quasisymmetry. Around it sits a ring of sharper, smaller questions stated verbatim in the literature: is a quasi-symmetry necessarily a Killing field; analyse BKM's necessary condition; convergence vs asymptoticity of the near-axis expansion; global realisability of |B| in Boozer coordinates; sheared vacuum fields; smooth sheared equilibria of arbitrary knot type; explicit solutions with irrational constant iota; the weakened (generic) Grad conjecture.

### Cited Findings

**Existence of exact quasisymmetry (the main problem).**
- "Finally, a related open question is the existence of non-axisymmetric equilibria satisfying exact quasisymmetry." (28 Sept 2026) [full text] — [Landreman, arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742)
- "It is unknown whether or not quasisymmetry can be achieved in a volume. We leave open the question of whether or not, using our approach, a carefully designed field xi (perhaps constructed dynamically along side the solution) can be used to ensure quasisymmetry on a flux surface or a volume." (2020) [full text] — [CDG, arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)
- "There remain open problems in this area. For example, the near-axis expansion of quasisymmetric equilibria results in an overdetermined system of equations at sufficiently high order. It remains to be seen if it is possible to circumvent the overdetermined nature with a modified formulation or if quasisymmetry can be obtained globally within a volume." (v2, Aug 2020; listed under "Theoretical understanding of quasisymmetry" in the closing section "New frontiers of optimization") [full text] — [Imbert-Gerard, Paul, Wright, "An Introduction to Stellarators: From magnetic fields to symmetries and optimization", arXiv:1908.05360](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05360)
- "It seems unlikely that this condition can be achieved exactly everywhere within the plasma (this remains to be proved rigorously), but in practice it is sufficient that it is attained to a good approximation." (2020; Helander is a co-author) [full text] — [Rodriguez, Helander, Bhattacharjee, arXiv:2004.11431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11431)
- "it is currently unknown whether exact QS can be achieved exactly in a toroidal volume for a magnetic field that satisfies the constraint of force balance in ideal MHD" (2023). [snippet, attributed by the search tool to a 2023 paper, probably arXiv:2302.13924; not verified in full text] — [arXiv:2302.13924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13924)

**Is a quasi-symmetry a Killing field?** "We have not managed to prove or disprove that a quasi-symmetry is a Killing field yet"; the necessary condition `u x V1 . V2 = 0` (Theorem 10.7) "merits analysis, but there are also the further restrictions given by (95) and (93) itself. And it would be good to also remove the MHS condition." [full text] — [BKM, arXiv:1912.06468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06468)

**Existence theory for the quasi-symmetric Grad-Shafranov system.** BKM note that even for the axisymmetric GS equation "there are regimes with no solutions", and thank colleagues for "discussion on the supplementary conditions for its quasi-symmetric generalisation"; no existence theorem is given for (93)-(97). [full text] — [arXiv:1912.06468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06468)

**Convergence or asymptotic nature of the near-axis expansion.**
- Weak-QS expansion to all orders: "We do not prove that the expansion converges (it is likely divergent but asymptotic)"; the construction "will also serve in the future as a test case for analysis of the convergence (or asymptotic) properties of the near-axis expansion". [abstract + full text] — [Rodriguez, Sengupta, Bhattacharjee, arXiv:2110.13703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13703)
- Vacuum case: ill-posedness proved, regularisation proposed; extension beyond vacuum left open ("we focus on a problem we know is solvable: Laplace's equation"). [full text] — [Ruth, Jorge, Bindel, arXiv:2411.04352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04352)
- A convergent axis expansion now exists in one closed-field-line MHS setting (de Lacy-Sato), where "this primitive involves no small denominators". [full text] — [arXiv:2611.16688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16688)
- Second-order QS: "we have not proved rigorously that solutions exist in which B20 is exactly constant". [full text] — [Landreman, Sengupta, arXiv:1908.10253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10253)

**Global realisability without force balance.** Klotz et al. settle the local analytic question and leave open "global realizability and satisfying force-balance conditions". [full text] — [arXiv:2609.09517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09517). Burby et al. solve "half" the problem (admissible symmetries). [abstract] — [arXiv:2403.03352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03352)

**Open questions listed by the 2026 counterexample papers.**
- Landreman: "Since at least three families of exact solutions exist, it is likely that there are others. Similar analytic solutions could be sought with other numbers of field periods, or with iota having a constant value that is irrational. If explicit solutions could be found that break stellarator symmetry ... It remains unknown whether exact analytic solutions exist for non-axisymmetric toroidal magnetic fields with nested toroidal flux surfaces if we demand the fields be curl-free (vacuum) or force-free." [full text] — [arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742). Status as of 26 Nov 2026: other field-period numbers answered by [arXiv:2611.07672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07672) (integer or zero iota); stellarator-symmetry breaking by [arXiv:2610.07304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07304); vacuum by [arXiv:2610.29921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29921); force-free by [arXiv:2611.31603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31603) [abstracts]. Irrational constant iota is achieved non-explicitly by EPP [arXiv:2611.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21574); I found no closed-form example.
- Lopez: "Whether a vacuum field without continuous Euclidean symmetry can fill a solid torus with nested toroidal flux surfaces and a sheared transform profile is open." [full text] — [arXiv:2610.29921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29921)
- EPP: with nonconstant rotational transform in arbitrary knot type, only finite regularity is obtained; C^infinity is not reached by their argument. [full text] — [arXiv:2611.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21574)
- Sengupta et al.: for the force-free family, "Their dynamical accessibility and their matching to the surrounding plasma remain open." [abstract] — [arXiv:2611.31603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31603)
- GLT relay DEG's question "whether all Euclidean symmetries, including the reflection, can be broken" (answered yes by GLT, de Lacy-Sato and EPP) and remark that systematic computer-assisted approaches "would nonetheless be interesting to systematically pursue". [full text] — [arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)

**Weakened Grad conjecture (generic boundaries and profiles).** Stated as still standing and unaddressed by the counterexamples. [full text] — [Burby, Cho, Ruth, arXiv:2610.36241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36241)

**Rigidity outside symmetry.** CDG (2021): rigidity results "for the full three-dimensional problem outside of symmetry ... seems to be out of reach of existing techniques"; Peralta-Salas-Slobodeanu (2026) give the first such theorem under the isodynamic condition and analyticity. [full text] — [arXiv:2007.09103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09103), [arXiv:2606.13462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13462)

**Quasi-isodynamic / omnigenous analogues.** Analytic exactly omnigenous fields must be quasisymmetric (Cary-Shasharina), so "the interesting cases ... are non-analytic"; near-axis omnigenity is constructed formally. [full text] — [Plunk, Landreman, Helander, arXiv:1909.08919](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08919). Second-order near-axis QI theory and a database programme are ongoing. [title seen in arXiv listing only for 2409.20328, "Near-axis description of stellarator-symmetric quasi-isodynamic stellarators to second order", Rodriguez, Plunk, Jorge, Sept 2024; abstract read for 2601.08400] — [arXiv:2409.20328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20328), [arXiv:2601.08400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08400)

**Anisotropic-pressure route.** Global exact-QS equilibria with anisotropic pressure are conjectured "likely" on the basis of formal expansions; the 6th-order numerical expansion diverges outside a small volume. [abstracts/full text] — [arXiv:2008.04715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04715), [arXiv:2505.20475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20475)

### Inferences
Candidate sub-problems, ordered roughly from most to least tractable for a non-specialist with an AI assistant and Lean 4. These rankings are my judgement, not statements from the literature.
1. **Formalise a closed-form counterexample in Lean 4.** Landreman's family 1 reduces to: (a) an axisymmetric polynomial-flux Solov'ev field satisfies `(B0.grad)B0 = -Dx`; (b) conjugation by a constant diagonal matrix commuting with D preserves this and div B = 0; (c) the vector identity `(curl B) x B = B.grad B - grad|B|^2/2`; (d) the level sets of psi in the stated domain are nested tori and B is nonvanishing; (e) no continuous Euclidean symmetry. Steps (a)-(c) are calculus identities; (d)-(e) need real work (topology of level sets, symmetry classification). Not novel mathematics, but a concrete, checkable contribution, and no formalisation of it is reported.
2. **Formalise the definitional layer of quasisymmetry**: equivalence of the formulations (CDG Def. 1, BKM Thm 4.2, Peralta-Salas-Slobodeanu Remark 1.3), BKM Theorems 7.3, 9.3, 10.5, 10.7, and the lemma "isodynamic implies weakly QS". These are finite differential-geometric computations with published proofs.
3. **Symbolic/AI-assisted search for exactly quasisymmetric members of the new explicit families** (or proofs that none exist there). Landreman names exact QS as open; the families are elementary; |B| is explicit. A negative result for a named family is a small, publishable, Lean-checkable lemma; a positive one would be a major result.
4. **BKM's necessary condition `u x V1 . V2 = 0`** and the "is a quasi-symmetry Killing?" question: a crisp algebraic-differential question. Peralta-Salas-Slobodeanu resolved the isodynamic analytic case in 27 pages by specialists, which calibrates the difficulty of even special cases.
5. **Finite-order solvability counts in the Garren-Boozer expansion**: making the third-order overdetermination into a theorem ("for an open dense set of axis shapes there is no third-order solution" or "the solvable set has codimension k") is a statement about ODE systems on a circle; I found no source claiming this has been proved rigorously.
6. **Convergence of a near-axis expansion** beyond the closed-field-line case: de Lacy-Sato's majorant argument works because there are no small denominators; with irrational transform this becomes a KAM-type problem (specialist territory).
7. **Exact QS existence or non-existence in a volume; the weakened Grad conjecture; sheared vacuum fields**: frontier research.

## 6. How hard are these by the field's own account? Is the main existence question within reach, or a problem on the scale of decades?

### Takeaway
By the field's own statements, Grad's conjecture was regarded in 2021 as beyond existing techniques and stood for 59 years, then fell in 2026 to a combination of a hard Nash-Moser construction and startlingly simple explicit solutions, both with AI assistance. For exact quasisymmetry the prevailing belief for 35 years has been non-existence in a volume (outside axisymmetry), with no proof either way; nobody I read gives a timescale, and after 2026 the sociological prior that such questions are decades away is visibly weaker, but the quasisymmetry problem is structurally harder because it is overdetermined.

### Cited Findings

[full text] — [arXiv:2007.09103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09103), [arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)

### Inferences
- The 2026 episode is direct evidence that a problem the field called "out of reach of existing techniques" in 2021 can fall quickly; but it fell in the *flexible* direction (constructing examples in an underdetermined problem). Exact quasisymmetry is *overdetermined*: one must either find a miraculous exact solution or prove a rigidity theorem. The one rigidity theorem available (isodynamic, analytic) needed analyticity and a 27-page specialist argument.
- The expert prior remains "no exact non-axisymmetric QS-MHS solutions in a volume", but several 2026 authors now list existence as an open question rather than asserting non-existence, and every pre-2026 heuristic for non-existence of smooth 3D equilibria was just shown to be unreliable.
- For a non-specialist with an AI assistant and Lean 4, the realistic contribution is not the main existence question but formalisation and exhaustive checking around it (section 5, items 1-3), in an area where specialist groups with frontier models are currently producing results within weeks; anything on the obvious extension list is at risk of being done first.
- No source supports a specific forecast ("years" vs "decades") for the quasisymmetry question; any such number would be speculation.

- I could not access expert commentary (blogs, talks, Simons reports) written after September 2026 on whether the counterexample techniques are expected to extend to quasisymmetry.
