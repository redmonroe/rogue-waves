## 1. Who is building or designing stellarators in 2024-2026, and on what design principles?

### Takeaway
At least six private companies plus the public programmes are designing reactor-scale stellarators, with roughly $1.0-1.3 billion of disclosed funding by late 2026 (my sum of company and trade-press figures); the published Proxima, Gauss and Type One designs are quasi-isodynamic (W7-X lineage), and only Thea Energy's Helios is built on quasisymmetry (quasi-axisymmetry). None of the sources seen reports a company-built stellarator confining plasma yet; all reactor timelines (early-to-mid 2030s) are company claims.

- Summing only the figures above (Proxima about $740 million company-stated total, which includes public money; Type One between $160 million and about $360 million depending on whether the $200 million Series B is additional to the earlier $160 million; Thea above $120 million in 2026 alone including the ARPA-E award; Renaissance about $34 million; Helical about $15-17 million), disclosed stellarator-company funding is roughly $1.0-1.3 billion by November 2026. ## 2. Do the designs rely on quasisymmetry or on quasi-isodynamic / omnigenous fields? Has emphasis shifted, and does exact-QS existence still matter?

### Takeaway
The centre of gravity of reactor design has moved to quasi-isodynamic fields (Proxima, Type One, Gauss, W7-X, and the largest open benchmark), with quasisymmetry retained mainly by Thea (QA) and the US academic line; by 2025-2026 the research frontier is moving further still, to piecewise-omnigenous and "symmetry-agnostic" fields that deliberately abandon exact symmetry. In that setting exact quasisymmetry is one idealised target among several, and nobody I found says a design decision hinges on whether it exists.

### Cited Findings
- QI designs: Stellaris ("high-field quasi-isodynamic"), Infinity Two ("quasi-isodynamic", four field periods), GIGA (modified W7-X equilibrium, QI metric) — sources as in section 1: [Proxima](https://www.proximafusion.com/press-news/proxima-fusion-and-partners-publish-stellaris-fusion-power-plant-concept-to-bring-limitless-safe-clean-energy-to-the-grid); [JPP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-plasma-physics/article/infinity-two-fusion-pilot-plant-baseline-plasma-physics-design/EFAA8FF6D37C95272E9F53AEFFE087A7); [arXiv 2607.09346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09346)
- QS designs: Helios (QA) [READ] — [arXiv 2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027); CFQS (QA), HSX (QH), STAR_Lite (QA), EPOS (QS) — sources in section 1
- Proxima's stated reason for QI (company-authored NeurIPS 2025 paper): QI configurations are "considered as a promising path to commercial fusion due to their inherent resilience to current-driven disruptions"; QI gives "a vanishing net plasma toroidal current" and "the advantages of even approximate QI fields have been validated ... most notably in the Wendelstein-7X (W7-X) stellarator" [READ] — [arXiv 2506.19583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19583)
- IPP theorists (Rodriguez and Plunk, January 2026) describe QI as having "advantageous properties over other omnigenous configurations (especially the celebrated quasisymmetric ones)", namely "the reduced level of bootstrap current ... and narrower particle orbits", and announce "a long term program to complete a systematic exploration of quasi-isodynamic stellarator design space" with a database of more than 800,000 near-axis QI configurations [READ] — [arXiv 2601.08400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08400)
- Thea's stated reason for QA (company claim): QA equilibria "can be more compact, their boundaries can be less strongly magnetically shaped, their coils can consequently be further away from the boundary"; and "for Helios, quasi-symmetry was found to be the most effective at producing equilibria which confine energetic particles" [READ] — [arXiv 2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)
- US policy framing as of 2020-2024: the FESAC Long Range Plan called the quasi-symmetric stellarator "the leading US approach to developing disruption-free, low-recirculating-power fusion configurations", as quoted in the 2024 facility white paper [READ] — [arXiv 2407.04039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04039)
- Piecewise omnigenity (CIEMAT with PPPL's F. Parra, 2024): "approaching omnigenity imposes severe constraints ... This, in turn, usually leads to complicated plasma shapes and coils"; the new family has "tokamak-like collisional energy transport while having transitioning particles" and "radically broadens the space of accessible reactor-relevant configurations". The same paper states "the complexity of the coils needed to produce a nearly QS field was the main reason for the cancellation of the NCSX project" [READ] — [arXiv 2405.07634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07634)
- pwO fields with zero bootstrap current proven to exist, "establish[ing] piecewise omnigenity as an alternative approach to stellarator reactor design" (Phys. Rev. E 112, L023201, 2025) [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2505.02546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02546); a March 2026 follow-up combines QI and pwO fields to ease integration with "other physical and technological aspects of a stellarator reactor" [SNIPPET] — [arXiv 2603.12377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12377)
- Princeton (Sengupta, Bhattacharjee, Buller, 20 August 2026): "Quasisymmetry, omnigenity and piecewise omnigenity confine trapped particles by making the bounce action independent of the field-line label. Recent optimizations produce mixed-symmetry stellarators that confine alpha particles well without them"; "direct fast-ion optimization can produce fields with good energetic-particle confinement without exact QS or O or pwO" [READ] — [arXiv 2608.20042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20042). Note that Bhattacharjee is the director of the Simons "Hidden Symmetries" collaboration.
- Landreman et al. (17 June 2026): Bayesian optimisation with particle tracing in the loop "yield[s] stellarator configurations with excellent fast-particle confinement in fields that can be far from quasisymmetric or quasi-isodynamic" [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2606.19523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19523). Landreman is the first author of the 2021-22 "precise quasisymmetry" result.
- Status of the exact-QS question in the physics literature: Landreman and Paul wrote that "it has been conjectured (but not proven) that in the absence of axisymmetry, QS may be possible on an isolated surface but it cannot be perfectly realized throughout a volume", and that their results "do not disprove the conjecture that QS cannot be achieved exactly throughout a volume" [READ] — [arXiv 2108.03711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03711)
- A May 2025 paper restates it: "The Garren-Boozer (GB) conundrum has been interpreted to mean that globally quasisymmetric magnetohydrostatic (MHS) equilibria, other than axisymmetric solutions, with isotropic pressure do not exist. When expanded as power series ... the governing equations become overdetermined at the 3rd order. Despite this, recent optimization efforts have found numerical isotropic-pressure equilibria with nearly exact global QS"; allowing pressure anisotropy "formally enables the study of equilibria with exact, global QS" [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2505.20475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20475)
- Rigorous partial results: Constantin, Drivas and Ginsberg construct smooth non-symmetric equilibria with nested surfaces that are "'nearly' quasisymmetric" and solve magnetohydrostatics "with a small force" (December 2020) [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860); Burby, Kallinikos, MacKay, Perrella and Pfefferlé "solve 'half' the problem of finding three-dimensional quasisymmetric magnetic fields that do not necessarily satisfy force balance" (March 2024) [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2403.03352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03352)
- My extended search (26 Nov 2026) found no 2025-2026 paper claiming to prove or disprove existence of exact non-axisymmetric quasisymmetric equilibria; the question still appears open [SNIPPET-level negative result] — [search result example: arXiv 2106.08266](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.08266v2)
- Publication-count signal [COUNT, physics archive incl. cross-lists, abstract field, by first-submission year; 2026 is year-to-date]: abstracts containing "quasi-isodynamic": 1 (2018), 6 (2022), 7 (2024), 11 (2025), 11 (2026); "omnigen*": 3, 4, 4, 6, 12; "quasisymm*": 1, 7, 11, 10, 10 (and 14 in 2020); "quasi-axisymmetric": 3, 4, 6, 6, 13; "quasi-helically": 0, 2, 3, 1, 11 — [arXiv advanced search](https://arxiv.org/search/advanced)

### Inferences
- Quasisymmetry has not been abandoned (Thea's whole design, CFQS, the QUASR database, and a steady ~10-25 QS-themed preprints a year), but it is no longer "the" target: the best-funded company (Proxima), the company with the most detailed peer-reviewed design series (Type One), Gauss, and the only large experiment are all QI. The QI/omnigenity literature grew from about 3 to about 23 abstracts a year between 2018 and 2026 while "quasisymm*" stayed flat near 10.
- The 2026 papers from the two people most associated with the QS programme (Landreman; Bhattacharjee's group) both argue that good alpha confinement can be had "without" or "far from" QS/QI. That is the strongest evidence I found that the field's own leaders regard exact symmetry as sufficient but unnecessary.
- Consequently the practical weight of "does exact QS exist?" has fallen since 2021: (a) Landreman-Paul made it moot numerically for QS; (b) most reactor designs do not target QS at all; (c) the newest design philosophies drop exact symmetry on purpose to buy coil simplicity. The analogous existence questions for exact QI or omnigenous equilibria may be equally open, but I did not research them.
- From background knowledge, not verified in this session: Type One Energy's earlier concepts drew on HSX-style quasi-helical symmetry before its published Infinity Two design adopted QI. If confirmed, that would be a concrete case of a company moving away from QS.

### Gaps
- No source found that directly surveys designers on "QS versus QI"; the shift is inferred from published designs and paper counts.
- I did not check whether Renaissance Fusion or the Chinese programme has committed to QS or QI.
- The arXiv counts miss journal-only papers and pure-mathematics (math.AP/math-ph-only) papers, and wildcard matching is imperfect; treat them as trends, not totals.

## 3. What limits stellarator reactor designs today, and would an existence or non-existence proof for exact QS change any engineering decision?

### Takeaway
Designers' own papers name turbulence, coil forces/tolerances/complexity, HTS magnet maturity, plasma-coil spacing for blankets, alpha-loss heat loads, divertors and tritium breeding as the binding constraints; collisionless confinement of the ideal field is treated as essentially solved, and intrinsic symmetry-breaking is already below what coils and manufacturing errors add. I found no evidence that a proof either way about exact quasisymmetry would alter a design choice; at most it would be of interest to the roughly ten-papers-a-year theory community.

### Cited Findings
- Landreman and Paul: for a 1 T field, "symmetry-breaking mode amplitudes throughout a volume of aspect ratio 6 can be made as small as the typical ~50 µT geomagnetic field"; "the deviations from QS intrinsic in the new QA configuration are small enough to be subdominant to extrinsic sources of field error" [READ] — [arXiv 2108.03711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03711)
- Thea's Helios accepts far-from-perfect confinement in practice: with real coils, "6.6% of the fusion product energy is simulated to be lost to the wall. While this is higher than some examples in academic literature, it is entirely sufficient for plasma self-heating and ignition"; the stated problem is that the loss "is highly peaked, with some areas receiving up to 4 MW/m^2 of heat flux; this is an area of ongoing optimization" [READ] — [arXiv 2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)
- Gauss's GIGA requirement is alpha power confinement "greater than 85%", i.e. up to 15% loss is acceptable in the design basis [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2607.09346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09346)
- Turbulence: Helios assumes confinement follows the ISS04 empirical scaling with a multiplier of 1.4 (1.33 in the simulated scenario) and says "this level of turbulent transport should also be justified via high-fidelity, first-principles simulation" [READ] — [arXiv 2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)
- Turbulence in W7-X: neoclassical optimisation worked, but turbulent transport clamps the central ion temperature near 1.5 keV in electron-heated plasmas (Beurskens et al. 2021) and "turbulence is the dominant transport mechanism in most discharges" (Ford et al. 2024); conference work warns that too much ITG turbulence could keep ion temperature below the burn point [SNIPPET] — [KIT repository copy](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000140617/134757371); [IAEA FEC contribution](https://conferences.iaea.org/event/214/contributions/17732/contribution.pdf)
- The US facility white paper lists the "specific scientific gaps on the stellarator path" as "energetic particle confinement, turbulent ion transport and core/divertor optimization", and wants to "test the physics basis of the potentially revolutionary non-resonant divertor" [READ] — [arXiv 2407.04039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04039)
- Coils: Helios's motivation is that HSX, NCSX and W7-X "all three exhibited cost and schedule overruns, and identified significant practical difficulty in designing, manufacturing, and assembling complexly curved, 3D coils to the required precision"; planar coils with individually powered shaping coils are said to relax tolerances (company claim) [READ] — [arXiv 2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)

- IAEA World Fusion Outlook 2026 (released 12 November 2026) is reported as citing an $80 billion private-capital gap before fusion power reaches the grid [SNIPPET; secondary coverage] — [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8577/iaea-publishes-2026-world-fusion-outlook/)

### Inferences
- Who would use what, stated bluntly:
  - A non-existence proof would confirm what practitioners already assume (the 1991 Garren-Boozer overdetermination argument) and change nothing in SIMSOPT/DESC/STELLOPT workflows, which minimise a symmetry-breaking residual and never require it to reach zero.
  - An existence proof (isotropic pressure, volume-filling, non-axisymmetric) would be a genuine mathematical surprise, but designers already reach symmetry-breaking of order 10^-5 of the field in the ideal boundary problem and then give most of it back to coil ripple, finite-build coils, 15 mm-scale structural deformation and assembly error. Helios tolerates 6.6% alpha energy loss; GIGA budgets up to 15%.
  - The users of constructive, approximate mathematics (near-axis expansions, databases, proxies) are real: optimiser seeding is documented at IPP, Princeton, Maryland and Flatiron. That is a different product from an existence theorem, and still further from a formalised (e.g. Lean) proof, for which I found no stated demand from any design team.
- The binding uncertainties are ones mathematics of exact symmetry does not address: turbulent transport, magnets, neutron-facing engineering, cost. Even within confinement theory, the active question in 2026 is "how little symmetry is enough", not "is perfect symmetry attainable".
- A conceivable indirect value: rigorous results about how symmetry-breaking must scale with aspect ratio or rotational transform could bound what optimisers can achieve (e.g. for compact QA designs). I found numerical explorations of that question (Nies et al. 2024, [arXiv 2404.02240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02240)) but no designer citing a theorem as a constraint.

### Gaps
- No designer statement was found that explicitly says "an existence proof would not matter"; the conclusion is inferred from what designers do and say about their real constraints. A direct question to Landreman, Helander/Plunk, or a company design lead would settle it.
- I did not read the Stellaris or Infinity Two risk sections directly, nor any formal independent review (e.g. the GIGA expert-panel report, DOE milestone review documents), none of which appear to be public.
- Cost breakdowns (share of plant cost in magnets versus blanket, etc.) were not found.

## 5. Have outsiders or small independent efforts contributed? Are there open benchmarks or leaderboards?

## 6. Weapons / dual-use check

### Takeaway
On the evidence found, magnetic-confinement field design is about as "peacetime" as fusion research gets: authoritative analyses locate fusion's proliferation relevance in neutrons (breeding fissile material in a blanket), tritium, and inertial-confinement knowledge, none of which depends on how the confining field is shaped. US regulators treat fusion machines like accelerators rather than reactors, and the IAEA applies no safeguards to fusion designs without nuclear material. The residual link is generic: any working DT power plant, stellarator or not, is a strong neutron source.

### Cited Findings
- Glaser and Goldston (Nuclear Fusion 52, 043004, 2012) analyse three magnetic-fusion scenarios — clandestine production in an undeclared facility, covert production in a declared plant, and breakout — and find proliferation risk "can be much lower than the equivalent risk from fission systems, if the fusion system is designed to accommodate appropriate safeguards"; a clandestine fusion breeder would be conspicuous through continuous power draw and dissipation of "at least some 40 MW"; covert fertile-material insertion would be easy for inspectors to detect; after breakout the minimum time to produce weapon-usable material is "one to two months" [SNIPPET of the abstract and PPPL report abstract] — [NASA ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012NucFu..52d3004G); [PPPL-4745 abstract](https://bp-pub.pppl.gov/pub_report/2012/PPPL-4745-abs.html); [IOP news summary](https://ioppublishing.org/news/fusion-presents-low-proliferation-risk-experts-conclude/)
- The same authors separately flag tritium and knowledge from inertial-fusion R&D as "vertical" proliferation concerns, and argued in 2011 that ignition at the National Ignition Facility could spur inertial-confinement R&D worldwide "along with increased proliferation risks" [SNIPPET] — [PPPL-4794 abstract](https://bp-pub.pppl.gov/pub_report/2012/PPPL-4794-abs.html); [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, 1 May 2011](https://thebulletin.org/?p=36940)
- Goldston's 2022 DOE briefing on fusion safeguards: a DT plant may hold kilograms of tritium per GWe and recycle kilograms per day per GWe; the safeguards goal is that grams per year are not diverted; tritium accountancy technology "will need improvement" [SNIPPET] — [DOE FES presentation](https://science.osti.gov/-/media/fes/pdf/fes-presentations/2022/Goldston-Fusion-Safeguards.pdf)
- A 2025 INMM panel presentation by Glaser reuses the 2012 reference scenario (about one significant quantity of plutonium in one month) and cites newer work on detecting covert production via antineutrinos [SNIPPET] — [Princeton SGS slides](https://sgs.princeton.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/Panel-REV1.pdf)
- Fissile breeding in a compact tokamak blanket (MIT; Ball, Peterson, Kemp, Ferry, April 2024): plants with neutron source rates above 10^20 n/s "could breed weapons-relevant quantities of fissile material on short timescales"; for an ARC-class FLiBe blanket "a significant quantity of fissile material can be bred in less than six months of full power operation" with 5-50 tonnes of fertile inventory, "a non-negligible proliferation risk"; lithium-6 enrichment "substantially reduces breeding rate" [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2404.12451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12451)
- Legal/technical review (Hua, Desai, Roma, Di Fulvio, Mundie, Pozzi, July 2022): the nuclear non-proliferation regime "legally ... does not apply" to fusion power plants; "fusion should continue to fall outside the nonproliferation regime" and dual-use export controls, possibly with "controls by design", are the better tool [READ abstract] — [arXiv 2207.14348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14348)
- IAEA position as presented at an IAEA DEMO workshop (2025): safeguards agreements cover designs that process, use or produce nuclear material; for fusion designs that do not, the IAEA "does not currently apply further measures" [SNIPPET of slides] — [IAEA conference slides](https://conferences.iaea.org/event/385/contributions/35197/attachments/19667/34488/DEMO_Special_Topic_Non_Prol_2025_LWP.pdf)
- Tritium is on the Nuclear Suppliers Group dual-use list (when the tritium-to-hydrogen atom ratio exceeds 1 in 1,000); tritium and lithium-6 are under US dual-use export controls [SNIPPET; note sources describe the list inconsistently as "Trigger List Part 2"] — [RSIS commentary, June 2026](https://rsis.edu.sg/rsis-publication/rsis/co26123/); [Hua and Di Fulvio paper copy](https://acdis.illinois.edu/sites/default/files/2026-01/Hua%20and%20DiFulvio%20Published%20Paper.pdf)
- More cautious voices: a Lawrence Livermore CGSR paper (April 2026) argues existing regimes "were not designed to account for fusion energy and all its proliferation risks" and proposes extending IAEA safeguards to large fusion facilities and updating NSG lists; an Atlantic Council paper (April 2025) addresses fusion non-proliferation and export considerations [SNIPPET] — [LLNL CGSR](https://cgsr.llnl.gov/sites/cgsr/files/2026-05/CGSR%20Lashanlo%20Publication_Final%20April%202026.pdf); [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Fusion-nonproliferation-and-export-considerations.pdf)
- US NRC: in April 2023 the Commission chose to regulate near-term fusion under the byproduct-material framework (10 CFR Part 30, as for accelerators) rather than as utilization facilities (reactors); the ADVANCE Act (9 July 2024) put a "fusion machine" definition into the Atomic Energy Act [SNIPPET] — [AIP FYI](https://www.aip.org/fyi/split-of-fusion-regulation-from-fission-codified-by-new-law); [NRC fusion machine page](https://www.nrc.gov/materials/fusion-machine)
- NRC proposed rule "Regulatory Framework for Fusion Machines", 91 FR 9476, 26 February 2026 (docket NRC-2023-0071); comments closed 27 May 2026; the NRC agenda targeted a final rule by October 2026, but I found no evidence of a published final rule as of 26 November 2026 [SNIPPET] — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/26/2026-03865/regulatory-framework-for-fusion-machines)

- Openness of the stellarator field in practice: the central QS and QI papers, codes and databases are on arXiv, GitHub and Hugging Face; NIFS has published all 27 years of LHD data; a QA stellarator (CFQS) is a China-Japan joint project [READ/SNIPPET as in earlier sections] — [arXiv 2506.19583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19583); [NIFS](https://www.nifs.ac.jp/en/info/251225e.html); [NIFS joint project](https://reso.nifs.ac.jp/nsjp/main.html)

### Inferences
- None of the proliferation analyses seen distinguishes tokamak from stellarator, and none identifies confinement-field mathematics as sensitive. The pathways are (i) neutrons captured in fertile material placed in or near the blanket, (ii) tritium diversion, (iii) inertial-confinement physics overlapping thermonuclear weapon design and stockpile stewardship. Stellarator quasisymmetry/omnigenity theory touches (i) and (ii) only in the trivial sense that it helps make a DT plant work at all, and does not touch (iii).
- A thermonuclear weapon needs no magnetic confinement; steady-state toroidal confinement physics has no route into weapon design. In that sense the mathematics is "peacetime" in the strict meaning of the question.
- Honest caveats: (a) a successful stellarator power plant is a >10^20 n/s neutron source, and Ball et al. show such a source can breed a significant quantity of fissile material in months if a state chooses to misuse a declared plant; steady-state operation is, if anything, convenient for that. This is a property of all DT fusion plants and is addressed by safeguards-by-design, not by restricting field-design theory. (b) HTS magnets and some superconducting-magnet classes are generally export-controlled dual-use hardware, but I found no source tying that to stellarator design mathematics. (c) Policy is not frozen: LLNL and others are pressing for wider controls on fusion facilities.
