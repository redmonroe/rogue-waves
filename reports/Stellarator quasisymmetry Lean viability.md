# Grad fell; exact quasisymmetry stays a long shot

Exact quasisymmetry is worth one strictly time-boxed attempt of two weekly usage resets, but it is not "plausibly solvable" in that window: the certain outputs are small, and the open problem itself is a low-odds bet. Grad's conjecture is no longer open. Between 21 September and 23 November 2026 at least seven preprints refuted it in its standard form, the first with a 567,000-line Lean 4 certification, and the existence of non-axisymmetric equilibria with exact quasisymmetry is the named problem left standing. That problem is peacetime, has a public Mathlib-only statement template to build on, and would be checkable by computer algebra and Lean if a positive example were found. Against it: the field has expected non-existence for 35 years, a non-existence proof is specialist analysis with no search shortcut, no fusion design decision depends on the answer, and no documented non-expert has yet carried a research-level result through to Lean. The coordinator's working pick (certify Landreman's closed-form family, define quasisymmetry in Lean, test and search the new explicit families) is the right shape, with three corrections found while writing this report: Landreman's own repository already contains SymPy and Mathematica checks of his equations, so the computer-algebra half of step (a) is a reproduction; his first family was found by one prompt and 20 minutes 34 seconds of model work, so the scarce input is the expert's problem statement, not compute; and a direct check run for this report indicates his first family is not quasisymmetric, so the value of step (c) lies entirely in the search, not the test. Recommended first milestone, one reset: a Mathlib-only Lean certificate of Landreman's first family, a quasisymmetry predicate with one passing and one failing test instance, a residual table for every published closed-form family, and a calibration run of Landreman's published prompt. Stop at the end of reset two unless a candidate's quasisymmetry residual vanishes identically.

*Evidence key used throughout.* **[read]**: a researcher on this project downloaded and read the paper, or cloned and read the repository. **[abstract]**: arXiv abstract only. **[snippet]**: web-search summary only; the page was not opened. **[relayed]**: told to a researcher, never seen. **[estimate]**: judgement, not a sourced fact. **[checked here]**: I ran or read it myself while writing this report; it is in neither the research notes nor any reviewed source. Every arXiv item dated September to November 2026 is an unrefereed preprint.

## Grad's conjecture fell in September; exact quasisymmetry is what survived

The magnetohydrostatic (MHS) problem asks for a divergence-free field B on a solid torus with (curl B) x B = grad p and B tangent to the boundary. Grad's conjecture, in the Constantin-Drivas-Ginsberg form that the 2026 papers target, said that any smooth, non-isolated equilibrium whose pressure has nested toroidal level sets must have plane-reflection, axial or helical symmetry ([arXiv:2007.09103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09103)) [read]. In 2021 those authors called rigidity outside symmetry "out of reach of existing techniques". It then fell twice in two days, by two unrelated methods.

**Gómez-Serrano, Liehr and Taylor** posted a 147-page Nash-Moser construction on 21 September 2026: smooth equilibria on embedded solid tori with nested pressure tori whose Euclidean symmetry group is exactly cyclic, forming continuous families, with the main theorem "verified in Lean 4 using its mathematical library Mathlib" and depending only on Lean's three standard axioms ([arXiv:2609.24739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24739)) [read]. One day later **Landreman** posted closed-form solutions in elementary functions: a family with rotational transform 2 everywhere and a second with sheared transform taking rational and irrational values, both with B nowhere zero and a nonplanar axis ([arXiv:2609.26742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742)) [read]. The cascade that followed answered most of Landreman's own list of open questions within weeks: stellarator symmetry broken ([arXiv:2610.07304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07304)) [abstract], a closed-form vacuum field ([arXiv:2610.29921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29921)) [read in part], any number of field periods ([arXiv:2611.07672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07672)) [abstract], a real-analytic equilibrium with trivial symmetry group via a convergent axis expansion ([arXiv:2611.16688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16688)) [read in part], smooth equilibria of every knot type with every field line dense for irrational slope ([arXiv:2611.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21574)) [read in part], and closed-form force-free fields ([arXiv:2611.31603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31603)) [abstract]. Nearly every one of these papers discloses frontier-model use. None has a journal reference yet, and the researchers did not read the proof bodies, so "refuted" here means "refuted by several independent unrefereed preprints, one machine-checked".

Two things remain open. Burby, Cho and Ruth accept that "Grad's conjecture is false" and state a weakened version: smooth solutions fail to exist for *generic* boundary shapes and profiles, which finite-dimensional families of examples do not touch ([arXiv:2610.36241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36241)) [read]. And Landreman closes his paper with: "a related open question is the existence of non-axisymmetric equilibria satisfying exact quasisymmetry." A date-sorted arXiv search on 26 November found no later preprint attacking it; absence from a search is not proof that nobody is working on it.

### What exact quasisymmetry asks, and why it is harder than Grad

Quasisymmetry has several formulations that agree only under conditions. The "weak" form asks for a non-vanishing field u, tangent to the boundary, with div u = 0, B x u = grad psi for some function psi, and u . grad|B| = 0; the "strong" form replaces the last condition, and coincides with the weak one for unforced toroidal equilibria on the strength of a private communication, not a cited theorem ([arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860)) [read]. Burby, Kallinikos and MacKay give an equilibrium-independent definition through guiding-centre motion and three Lie-derivative conditions ([arXiv:1912.06468](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06468)) [read]. The famous open problem is the conjunction: quasisymmetry, plus isotropic force balance, throughout a toroidal volume, with u not a Euclidean symmetry.

The rigorous record on that conjunction is thin on both sides. Existence results all drop something: force balance (Sato's explicit weakly quasisymmetric fields, [arXiv:2204.09241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09241) [abstract]), exactness of force balance (a small residual force, [arXiv:2009.08860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08860) [read]), or isotropy of pressure ([arXiv:2008.04715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04715)) [abstract]. The case for non-existence is the Garren-Boozer near-axis expansion, which becomes overdetermined at third order in distance from the axis; the researchers read this only through later papers, and those papers themselves say overdetermination "cannot, by itself, be regarded as a proof of non-existence". The one genuine rigidity theorem is from June 2026: a real-analytic *isodynamic* equilibrium (a sub-case of weak quasisymmetry) must be axisymmetric, in a 27-page specialist argument that needs analyticity ([arXiv:2606.13462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13462)) [read].

Numerics do not settle it. Landreman and Paul reached symmetry-breaking of about 50 microtesla in a 1 tesla field at aspect ratio 6, but wrote that it "did not seem possible in this study to reduce symmetry-breaking errors down to machine precision" ([arXiv:2108.03711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03711)) [read]. The residual follows the cubic aspect-ratio scaling that Garren-Boozer predicts, and the equilibrium codes "do not actually solve the MHS equations" but resolution-dependent regularisations of them ([arXiv:2610.36241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36241)) [read]. The data fit an asymptotic near-miss as well as they fit an exact solution.

The structural point matters for tractability. Grad's conjecture fell in the *flexible* direction: the problem was underdetermined, and examples existed to be found. Exact quasisymmetry is *overdetermined*. One must either find a miraculous exact solution or prove rigidity. The 2026 counterexamples share a feature that made them findable (all field lines closed, or constant rotational transform, or a special integrable structure), and none is claimed to be quasisymmetric. The expert prior remains "no such equilibria outside axisymmetry"; what changed in 2026 is that every earlier heuristic against smooth three-dimensional equilibria was shown to be unreliable, and several authors now list existence as open instead of asserting the negative. No source gives a timescale.

## Designers moved to quasi-isodynamic fields, and no design decision waits on this theorem

Real, funded users of stellarator field design exist. The researchers' sum of company and trade-press figures puts disclosed stellarator-company funding near $1.0 to 1.3 billion by late 2026, but that arithmetic rests mostly on snippets and carries a double-counting risk. What is firmer is which principle each design uses. Proxima's Stellaris, Type One's Infinity Two and Gauss's GIGA are quasi-isodynamic, in the Wendelstein 7-X line ([arXiv:2607.09346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09346) [abstract]; the Stellaris and Infinity Two papers were seen only as snippets). Only Thea Energy's Helios is built on quasisymmetry: a two-field-period quasi-axisymmetric equilibrium at aspect ratio 4.5 ([arXiv:2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)) [read]. Type One's lead theorist is quoted as saying "we couldn't make quasi-symmetry work as well as we thought we could… it looks like QI is the prominent optimization choice" ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/stellarator)) [snippet]. The largest open benchmark, ConStellaration, is entirely quasi-isodynamic ([arXiv:2506.19583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19583)) [read].

The frontier has moved further from exact symmetry, led by the people most associated with it. Landreman and co-authors report configurations with excellent fast-particle confinement "in fields that can be far from quasisymmetric or quasi-isodynamic" ([arXiv:2606.19523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19523)) [abstract]. The Princeton group whose director led the Simons "Hidden Symmetries" collaboration writes that mixed-symmetry stellarators "confine alpha particles well without" quasisymmetry, omnigenity or piecewise omnigenity ([arXiv:2608.20042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20042)) [read]. That collaboration's funding period ended in August 2025 ([Simons Foundation](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/event/simons-collaboration-on-hidden-symmetries-and-fusion-energy-annual-meeting-2025/)) [snippet].

Would a proof change an engineering decision? **Nothing found says it would.** Optimisers minimise a symmetry-breaking residual and never require it to reach zero. Landreman and Paul's intrinsic error is already "subdominant to extrinsic sources of field error". Thea's Helios tolerates 6.6% alpha-energy loss with real coils and calls that "entirely sufficient" ([arXiv:2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)) [read]; Gauss budgets up to 15%. An independent study estimates Stellaris coils deform by about 15 mm under load ([arXiv:2507.12681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12681)) [read]. The binding constraints designers name are turbulence, coil forces and tolerances, magnet maturity, blanket spacing and divertors. This conclusion is an inference from what designers publish; no designer statement was found that says "an existence proof would not matter", and a direct question to Landreman or a company design lead would settle it.

The users that do exist are narrower. A non-existence proof would interest the roughly ten-papers-a-year quasisymmetry theory community (the researchers' own arXiv count, [arXiv advanced search](https://arxiv.org/search/advanced)) and confirm what practitioners assume. An existence proof would be a genuine mathematical surprise. Exact closed-form equilibria have one concrete practical use, which Landreman states: they "will be practically useful as tests of numerical equilibrium codes". Demand for *formal verification* from the plasma side is new and thin: it is inferred from three 2026 papers, and no stellarator group was found asking for it.

On the peacetime criterion the topic passes cleanly. Proliferation analyses locate fusion's weapons relevance in neutrons breeding fissile material in a blanket, in tritium, and in inertial-confinement physics; none distinguishes tokamak from stellarator or identifies confinement-field mathematics as sensitive ([Glaser and Goldston 2012](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012NucFu..52d3004G) [snippet]; [arXiv:2404.12451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12451) [abstract]; [arXiv:2207.14348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14348) [abstract]). The honest caveat is generic: any working deuterium-tritium plant is a strong neutron source, and the mathematics helps such a plant exist at all.

## Lean can state the problem today; the proofs are cheap and the statements are where projects fail

### Mathlib has the pointwise calculus and none of the global theory

A researcher audited a Mathlib checkout dated 19 November 2026 by grep and `#check` ([mathlib4](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4)) [read]. The result separates cleanly into what a pointwise identity needs and what an existence theorem needs.

| Status in Mathlib | Items |
|---|---|
| Present | Cross product on R^3 with identities; `gradient`; Fréchet derivatives and second-derivative symmetry; Lie bracket of vector fields; Picard-Lindelöf and integral curves; formal and multivariate power series with derivations; Fourier series on tori; analytic functions and the implicit function theorem; smooth manifolds; Riemannian metrics and a Levi-Civita connection |
| Partial | Exterior derivative (normed spaces only, not manifolds); divergence theorem (rectangular boxes only); Sobolev spaces (whole space only); tori (topological and measure level) |
| Absent | Named `curl` and `div`; Lie derivative; Killing fields; flows as diffeomorphisms with smooth parameter dependence; Frobenius; Cauchy-Kovalevskaya; elliptic regularity; KAM; symplectic and Hamiltonian mechanics; Nash-Moser |

Two scratch probes compiled on the first attempt: hand-rolled `grad`, `div`, `curl` with `div (curl B) = 0` in about 45 lines, and the divergence-free property of the axisymmetric seed field of Landreman's solutions in about 45 more. So checking an explicit field is cheap. Anything global (flux coordinates, Boozer angles, rotational transform, averaging over tori) has no ready vocabulary, and existence theory has nothing. Missing infrastructure is a cost, not a blocker: every large 2026 formalisation states its theorem in a short Mathlib-only file with hand-rolled operators and builds the analysis privately.

### Plasma mathematics was formalised for the first time in 2026

The Grad certification is the largest item and is exactly on topic. Its repository holds 5,847 Lean files and 566,654 lines, of which the trusted statement file `Showcase.lean` is 242 lines, imports only Mathlib, and defines `IsMHS`, embedded tori, nested pressure tori and the symmetry group directly ([lukasliehr/Grad-Conjecture](https://github.com/lukasliehr/Grad-Conjecture)) [read]. Its `Internal/` directory of supporting analysis (weighted function spaces, weak derivatives, tame estimates, the inverse of the linearised operator) is 517,000 lines. Plasma kinetics has a 10,000-line Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau formalisation ([arXiv:2603.15929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15929)) [read] and a relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell entry in OpenAI's library ([openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math)) [read]. Physlib has electromagnetism and a small fluids module but nothing on plasma or MHD ([physlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib)) [read]. **Quasisymmetry, Boozer coordinates, Grad-Shafranov and the near-axis expansion are formalised nowhere the researchers looked**: zero hits in Physlib, in OpenAI's catalogue, and in Google DeepMind's formal-conjectures collection ([formal-conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures)) [read]. That is an unoccupied niche, with the usual caveat about keyword searches.

### Compilation is not correctness, and this project reproduced the standard bug

The documented failures of AI-assisted formalisation are in statements and definitions, not proofs. In the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau project the main theorem "had accumulated 42 hypotheses" before being cut to 12, and after release a Mathlib maintainer found within one line that `ContDiff ℝ ⊤` means analytic in current Mathlib, not smooth as intended: "if there is one in the only line I've checked, how many others are there?" ([arXiv:2603.15929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15929)) [read]. A formalisation of Grothendieck's vanishing theorem "compiles with no sorries, but an expert review found serious problems in definitions, theorem generality, file organization, and the API" ([arXiv:2606.13925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13925)) [abstract]. A November 2026 paper states the position directly: "the risks lie in the statements rather than in the proofs" ([arXiv:2611.06962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06962)) [abstract].

This is not hypothetical here. The researcher's own scratch `IsMHS` structure wrote `⊤` intending "smooth", silently demanded real-analyticity, and compiled without complaint. For quasisymmetry the exposure is larger than for the zeta results, whose statements are short and written against Mathlib's existing `riemannZeta`: there are several inequivalent definitions, a choice of where B is non-zero, a regularity class, and the meaning of "non-axisymmetric". A technically capable non-mathematician can run the loop but cannot supply the expert read of the statement file, so that read has to be obtained elsewhere: a plasma theorist, the Lean Zulip, adversarial model review, and non-vacuity theorems that exhibit an object satisfying every hypothesis.

Mathlib is closed to this project as described. Its contribution guide says AI-written code without a Lean expert's supervision "fails to meet that bar by a large margin" and that such PRs will be closed without comment ([contribution guide source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/leanprover-community.github.io/blob/lean4/templates/contribute/index.md)) [read]. The open doors are a standalone repository in the trusted-statement, untrusted-proof layout that the lab releases use, PhyslibAlpha, and Tau Ceti, an AI-maintained downstream library ([TauCeti](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti)) [read: file names and one file only].

### Three classes of work, with very different evidence for individuals

**Class (a), re-formalising or compressing an existing machine-checked proof.** Evidence that an individual can do this is weak but positive. A small Anthropic group compressed the Riemann Hypothesis proof to a 76,000-line compact edition within days of its release ([lsernau/grh-lean](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean)) [read]; the original's size of about 17 million lines is known here only from a snippet. The story of an individual on a $100 plan getting "70% of the way" is **[relayed]**: a Hacker News self-report that neither researcher could find, and it concerns re-deriving a compressed version of an already-checked proof, not an open problem.

**Class (b), discovering a new proof of an open statement.** Done by labs with unreleased models; by experts steering released models, including in this exact subfield; and once by an amateur on a short Erdős problem whose raw output experts had to decode. **No documented case was found of a non-expert carrying a discovery through to Lean.** Exact quasisymmetry existence is class (b).

**Class (c), formalising a known proof where Mathlib lacks infrastructure.** Done repeatedly in 2026, always with an expert reading the statements. The closest precedent for an individual is the Vlasov-Maxwell-Landau project: one person, a $200-a-month Claude Max plan, 10 days, about 50 supervised hours, zero hand-written lines. He is a professional mathematician and still shipped a statement bug. Class (c) is positive for experts and **untested for non-experts**. Certifying Landreman's family and defining quasisymmetry are light class (c).

Three readings follow. First, the famous number-theory results are lab products: unreleased models, undisclosed or very large compute, staff mathematicians. They do not transfer to a subscription. Second, **the stellarator results are the opposite case**: released consumer-tier models and trivial compute. Landreman's published chat shows his first family arriving from a single carefully worded prompt in 20 minutes. What he supplied was the formulation, including three physical mechanisms for generating rotational transform, and the judgement to verify every equation. Model capability sufficient for discovery in this subfield is therefore plausibly available on a paid plan; the tier and volume each paper needed were not verified. Third, the autonomous base rates for hard open problems with public models were 0 to 3% per problem in September 2026. Models have improved since by an amount no source quantifies.

A caveat on model choice: the published closed-form discoveries used GPT-6 Astra Pro or Ultra or Gemini 4. Claude models are documented for proof detail and Lean code in the Grad certification and as part of Lopez's discovery team, not as the sole discoverer of a closed-form family. A calibration run is the way to find out, and Landreman's exact prompts are public.

## The strongest objections are structural, and each survives scrutiny

**The expected answer is "no", and "no" cannot be found by search.** Thirty-five years of expert belief point to non-existence outside axisymmetry. A non-existence result is a rigidity theorem, and the only one available needed analyticity, a sub-case, and specialists. A model-driven ansatz search can deliver a positive example or nothing; "nothing" carries no information unless the model was first shown able to rediscover a known family. The steelman is real but limited: the same expert community was wrong about smooth three-dimensional equilibria in general, and Klotz, Duignan, Burby and Meiss showed in September 2026 that there is no *local analytic* obstruction to prescribing field strength in Boozer coordinates when force balance is dropped ([arXiv:2609.09517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09517)) [read]. Force balance is exactly what the open problem adds.

**The person best placed to find an explicit example named the problem open instead of solving it.** Landreman had the model, the prompt-craft and explicit formulas for |B|. His three published chats never mention quasisymmetry [checked here], so there is no public record that the obvious prompt was tried, and whether he tried it privately is unknown. A check run for this report (script in the appendix) indicates his first family is not quasisymmetric: the standard triple-product residual grad psi x grad|B|^2 . grad(B . grad|B|^2) vanishes at epsilon = 0, as it must for the axisymmetric member, and is of order 0.01 to 6 at sampled points for epsilon = 1/4, 1/2 and 7/10. Because that family has closed field lines, the flux function in the definition is not unique, so a second test that does not depend on the choice of flux function was run at epsilon = 1/4 and was also non-zero. This is a point evaluation of exact symbolic derivatives, not a proof for all parameters, not reviewed, and not in Lean. It is enough to say that testing the published families is a matter of minutes to hours each and is likely to come back negative.

**The result would change no design decision.** Covered above. If practical consequence is a requirement, this target fails it. The only applied payoff in reach is exact benchmarks for equilibrium codes, which the September to November papers already supply.

**The subfield is crowded with specialists using the same or stronger tools.** At least seven counterexample preprints from several groups in nine weeks; most of Landreman's open list was consumed within eight. Any obvious next step should be assumed to be under attack already.

**Statement risk has no in-house mitigation.** The expert read of the statement file is the one ingredient every credible project had and this one lacks. A fast "proof" of non-existence from the model would almost certainly be a vacuous or mis-stated theorem.

**Reception is harder than in 2025.** arXiv tightened endorsement in January 2026 and announced one-year bans for unchecked AI content in May ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/research-repository-arxiv-will-ban-authors-for-a-year-if-they-let-ai-do-all-the-work)) [snippet]. Even at lab scale, OpenAI withdrew papers over "a shared sign error" ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/openai-just-changed-math-forever-exactly-how-no-one-yet-knows)) [snippet].

**Two weekly resets is short.** The only hard calibrations are Ilin's 10 days for 10,000 lines on a top-tier plan with an older model, and Landreman's 20 minutes for a discovery. Certification and a search fit in two resets. A resolution of the main question does not, except by luck.

## Ranked targets: certify first, then one bounded search

### The coordinator's pick is sound once its three parts are re-weighted

Part (a), verifying Landreman's family by computer algebra and certifying it in Lean, is the correct opening move and less novel than assumed. His supplemental repository already holds SymPy and Mathematica notebooks confirming div B = 0, B . grad psi = 0 and force balance for both families ([analytic_3d_equilibria](https://github.com/landreman/analytic_3d_equilibria)) [checked here], so the computer-algebra step is an hour of reproduction. The Lean certificate is new as far as anyone found. Keep it to the local identities; the topological claim that the level sets are nested tori is the main risk in the researcher's estimate and is not needed for the search.

Part (b), defining quasisymmetry in Lean, is where the project is most likely to go quietly wrong and where it has the clearest standalone value, because nobody has done it. State the weak form and the triple-product form, port the `IsMHS` definitions from the Grad statement file (pinned to Lean 4.33.1; current Mathlib is on 4.37), and test it from both sides. The axisymmetric member must satisfy the predicate, and that is a Lean theorem within reach of the first reset. The check above supplies an instance on the other side: Landreman's first family at epsilon > 0 should fail it. Proving that failure in Lean means showing no admissible u exists, which is harder than the positive instance and can stay at the computer-algebra level at first. A definition that both instances respect has been tested in the only way available without an expert. The one-to-four-week figure in the table below is the researcher's estimate for a reviewed statement layer, not for this first draft.

Part (c) should be split. *Testing* the published closed-form families is cheap and is expected to be negative; its output is a small, checkable lemma, which is a minor contribution by the user's own standard. *Searching* for a quasisymmetric member in wider ansatz spaces is the outright attempt, and it is the only part of the pick that meets the user's stated ambition. Families given only by existence proofs (Gómez-Serrano et al.; Enciso et al.) have no formula and cannot be tested this way.

## Conclusion

The question changed underneath the brief. In August the pitch would have been "Grad's conjecture and quasisymmetry"; by late November the first half is a certified theorem with seven sequels, and what is left is the overdetermined half that the same tools did not crack. That asymmetry is the finding. The 2026 stellarator results show that a released model on a paid plan can find new exact equilibria in minutes when an expert states the problem well, and that Lean certification of an explicit field is a small job. They do not show that the next problem on the list yields the same way, and a first direct check suggests the easiest place to look is empty.

So the verdict is a bounded yes. Run it for two resets as a bet whose floor is useful and whose ceiling is improbable: a first Lean certificate of an AI-discovered closed-form equilibrium, the first formal definition of quasisymmetry with tested instances, and a table of residuals that tells the field which of its new families are not quasisymmetric. **First milestone, end of reset one:** the Mathlib-only Lean certificate of Landreman's first family; the quasisymmetry predicate with its passing and failing instances; residuals for every published closed-form family; and the calibration run of Landreman's own prompt. Then send the statement file to the authors who posed the question, because their reading of it is worth more than another week of compute. If reset two ends without a candidate whose residual vanishes identically, stop, publish the certificate and the negative table, and take the next attempt to a problem whose Lean statement someone else has already written.

## Appendix: the quasisymmetry check run for this report

This script was run on 26 November 2026 with SymPy in an existing project environment. It first confirms the transcription of Landreman's equations (2.1) to (2.4): div B, the force-balance residual and B . grad psi evaluate to zero to more than 150 digits at every sampled point. It then evaluates the triple-product residual. Output: zero to working precision at epsilon = 0; values between 0.015 and 6.1 in magnitude for epsilon = 1/4, 1/2, 7/10. It runs in about three seconds. The working copies are at `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/f7c08234-634f-5a50-bfc5-78c71b1b878c/scratchpad/qs_check/qs_family1.py` and `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/f7c08234-634f-5a50-bfc5-78c71b1b878c/scratchpad/qs_check/qs_family1_frobenius.py`; that directory is temporary, so the first script is reproduced here. The second script forms N = B x (grad|B|^2 x grad(B . grad|B|^2)) and evaluates N . curl N, which must vanish wherever any weak quasisymmetry exists; it returned zero at epsilon = 0 and values of -2.7, -0.23 and 21.1 at three points for epsilon = 1/4, and timed out after seven minutes before reaching epsilon = 1/2.

```python
import sympy as sp
x, y, z, eps = sp.symbols('x y z epsilon', real=True)
a, b = sp.sqrt(1 + eps), sp.sqrt(1 - eps)
s = x**2/a**2 + y**2/b**2
F = sp.sqrt(1 - (1 - s)**2 - 4*z**2)
B = sp.Matrix([(2*z*x - (a/b)*F*y)/s, (2*z*y + (b/a)*F*x)/s, 1 - s])
X = (x, y, z)
grad = lambda f: sp.Matrix([sp.diff(f, v) for v in X])
div = lambda V: sum(sp.diff(V[i], X[i]) for i in range(3))
curl = lambda V: sp.Matrix([sp.diff(V[2], y) - sp.diff(V[1], z),
                            sp.diff(V[0], z) - sp.diff(V[2], x),
                            sp.diff(V[1], x) - sp.diff(V[0], y)])
B2 = (B.T*B)[0]
psi = (x**2 + y**2 + 4*z**2 + B2 - 2 + eps**2)/4
p = -2*psi
force = curl(B).cross(B) - grad(p)
BgB2 = (B.T*grad(B2))[0]
triple = grad(psi).cross(grad(B2)).dot(grad(BgB2))
R = sp.Rational
pts = [(R(11,10), R(1,7), R(1,9)), (R(-3,5), R(4,5), R(-1,8)),
       (R(1,3), R(-9,10), R(1,20)), (R(9,10), R(-1,2), R(1,6))]
for e in [0, R(1,4), R(1,2), R(7,10)]:
    for q in pts:
        sub = {**dict(zip(X, q)), eps: e}
        if (1 - (1 - s)**2 - 4*z**2).subs(sub) <= 0:
            continue  # outside the domain U_eps
        ev = lambda expr: sp.N(expr.subs(sub), 40)
        print(e, q, ev(div(B)), max(abs(ev(c)) for c in force),
              ev((B.T*grad(psi))[0]), ev(triple))
```
